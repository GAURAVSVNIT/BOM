# How an FIBC Bulk Bag Is Manufactured

> **Scope note.** Everything below is recorded ONLY from nationalbulkbag.com and
> blog.nationalbulkbag.com. National Bulk Bag is a **distributor**, not a manufacturer —
> its content is written for buyers, not for process engineers. As a result the site
> documents *what the finished bag is* in considerable detail, but documents *how the
> factory makes it* only in fragments. Stages marked `GAP` are genuinely absent from
> the site; they are recorded as gaps rather than filled from outside knowledge.

**Where manufacturing happens:** "the vast majority of bulk bags distributed in the US
are made overseas"
([USA vs Overseas](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/fibc-bulk-bags-manufacturing-usa-versus-overseas)).
"Most bags are sourced overseas, most notably in Asia (China, India)," with Mexico a
secondary source that "essentially cuts lead times in half"
([Lead Times](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/what-impacts-fibc-jumbo-bag-lead-times)).
The stated driver is labor cost: "The cost of labor to produce FIBCs overseas is
significantly less than in the US," and even after logistics "overseas bags are still
significantly less expensive than bags manufactured in the US"
([USA vs Overseas](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/fibc-bulk-bags-manufacturing-usa-versus-overseas)).

## Stage 1 — Raw material: polypropylene resin and additives

**Base polymer.** FIBC fabric is polypropylene. The glossary describes polypropylene as
"a thermoplastic resin material created by the polymerization of propylene"
([FIBC Bulk Bag Glossary](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/)).

**Virgin vs. recycled.** Food-grade bags "must be manufactured with 100% virgin
polypropylene resin," which "insure[s] that no recycled materials are included,"
meeting "US FDA Food Contact Regulation 21 CFR 177.1520"
([Food Grade Myths and Facts](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/food-grade-bulk-bags-3-myths-and-facts);
[Top 5 Things to Look For](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/top-5-things-to-look-for-when-selecting-a-food-grade-fibc-supplier)).
The vocabulary guide defines it: "100% virgin resin, used in the manufacturing of food
grade bags is polypropylene resin that has never been used"
([FIBC Vocabulary](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/a-guide-to-flexible-intermediate-bulk-container-vocabulary)).
Fusion bags are stated to use "all virgin materials and approved for direct food contact"
([Why Fusion Bulk Bags Are Built Differently](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/why-fusion-bulk-bags-are-built-differently)).

**UV stabilizer — added at the resin stage, before extrusion.** This is the single most
explicit statement on the site about additive sequencing:

> "UV Stabilized — A feature of an FIBC fabric that provides protection from prolonged
> exposure to the sun's degrading UV rays. **An additive is added to the resin prior to
> extrusion of the yarns** to provide this protection."
> — [FIBC Bulk Bag Glossary](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/)

The UV article adds: "When FIBCs are made, the polypropylene is mixed with polymer
stabilizers" — a "class of chemical additives" that "inhibit degradation of the material"
by absorbing UV rays and converting them into low-level heat. The additives are not
named chemically. Stabilizers "do break down over time"
([What is UV Stabilization?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-construction/what-is-uv-stabilization-how-does-it-impact-fibc-bulk-bags)).

**Flame-retardant additive — also added at the resin stage.** "During the manufacturing
process of halogenated FIBCs, halogens are added to the resin creating a halogenated
flame-retardant bulk bag." The halogens are "fluorine, chlorine, bromine, iodine, and
astatine," and they "act in the 'vapor phase' of flames and interfere with the physical
chemistry of the fire source itself, retarding the process." Trade-off stated: "When
halogenated materials burn, they may release toxic materials that can be harmful or even
fatal to bystanders." Flame-retardant bags come "in both halogenated and non-halogenated
versions"
([What is Halogenation?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-construction/what-is-halogenation-fibc-bulk-bags)).

**GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com** — resin grade / melt flow index; resin
pellet handling, drying, or blending; masterbatch dosing; antistatic additive chemistry;
colorant/pigment addition; recycled-content percentages in non-food bags.

## Stage 2 — Extrusion: resin to tape/yarn

The site confirms that extrusion of yarns happens and that it happens *after* additives
are compounded into the resin — "An additive is added to the resin prior to extrusion of
the yarns" ([FIBC Bulk Bag Glossary](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/)).
The output of extrusion is described only in end-product terms: woven PP is made from
"strips or threads of poly"
([All About Woven Polypropylene Bags](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/all-about-woven-polypropylene-bags))
and from "strands of polypropylene"
([What are Breathable Bulk Bags?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/what-are-breathable-bulk-bags/)).

**GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com** — melting/extruder temperatures; cast film
or blown film production; film slitting into tapes; hot-air oven stretching and molecular
orientation; draw ratio; annealing; tape width; **denier** (the term does not appear
anywhere on the site); winding onto bobbins/spools; extrusion line throughput.

## Stage 3 — Weaving

**The basic operation.** "Woven polypropylene is created by taking strips or threads of
poly and weaving them together in opposite directions to form a strong, lightweight
material"
([All About Woven Polypropylene Bags](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/all-about-woven-polypropylene-bags)).
Elsewhere: FIBCs are constructed by "weaving strands of polypropylene together to form a
sturdy mesh"
([What are Breathable Bulk Bags?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/what-are-breathable-bulk-bags/)),
and "Uncoated FIBCs are constructed by weaving together strands of polypropylene"
([Coated vs Uncoated](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/how-to-choose-between-coated-and-uncoated-fibc-bulk-bags-for-your-application)).
The site attributes the material's durability to the weave: "Their woven nature makes WPP
bags very durable and resistant to tears, bursting, or punctures."

**Tubular (circular) fabric vs. panel fabric.** The site distinguishes fabric *forms*
even though it never names the loom types:
- Circular bags "are made with a tubular piece of fabric that forms all sides of the bag
  allowing for stitching only for the top and bottom"
  ([Types of Jumbo Bag Construction](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/types-of-jumbo-bag-construction-upanel-circular-4panel));
  restated as "one 'tubular' piece[] of fabric that forms all sides of the bag allowing
  for the stitching only at the top and bottom"
  ([Different Types of FIBC Construction](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/what-are-the-different-types-fibc-construction)).
- Cross-corner lift loops "require that the bag is made from circular fabric"
  ([Types of Lift Loops](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/fibc-bulk-bag-101-types-of-lift-loops-explained))
  — i.e. loop style is constrained by which fabric form was woven.

**Vented / breathable fabric is created at the loom.** "Vented bags have strips that run
vertically along each side. These strips have small strands of polypropylene that are
**not woven**, allowing much more air to pass between the fabric." Uncoated bags "breathe
naturally to a certain extent" because of the weave gaps
([What are Breathable Bulk Bags?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/what-are-breathable-bulk-bags/)).

**Conductive/antistatic properties are woven in, not applied.**
- Type A: "plain-woven polypropylene and other non-conductive fabrics."
- Type B: "plain-woven polypropylene fabrics or other non-conductive material" engineered
  with low breakdown voltage.
- Type C: "non-conductive polypropylene fabrics **interwoven with conducting threads**
  traditionally sewn in a grid pattern"; the threads must be "electrically interconnected
  as well as connected to a designated ground/earth."
- Type D: "antistatic and static dissipative fabrics" using "fabric containing
  **quasi-conductive yarns**," dissipating via "safe, low-energy corona discharge" with
  no external grounding.
([Types of FIBCs: A B C & D Explained](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/types-of-fibc-bulk-bags-a-b-c-d-explained/))

**Fabric weight is the key woven-fabric spec quoted.**
> "Fabric Weight — The measure of the fabric weight in ounces per square yard or grams
> per 100 square centimeters. **A typical standard weight is 6.5-ozs/sq. yd.**"
> — [FIBC Bulk Bag Glossary](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/)

The vocabulary guide gives the units as "grams per meter squared and ounces per square
yard"
([FIBC Vocabulary](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/a-guide-to-flexible-intermediate-bulk-container-vocabulary)).
WPP produce-bag options are listed as including "fabric weight (GSM), bag construction,
seam style, top construction, and custom printing"
([Woven Polypropylene Bags](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/produce-packaging/woven-polypropylene-bags/)).

**Specialty woven fabric replacing a construction step.** Hyperbolic™ baffle bags "ditch
the internal baffles for a specially woven fabric" that "manages outward forces,
preserving the shape without additional seams" — reported as **"20% Less Manpower in
Manufacturing," "23% Less Material Consumption," "Zero Vertical Seams,"** and "30%
Storage Optimization"
([Baffle Bags Transformed](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/baffle-bags-transformed)).

**GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com** — the words "circular loom" and "flat loom"
do not appear; warp/weft terminology; mesh count, picks per inch, ends per inch; loom
speed or shuttle count; fabric roll widths; inspection of woven fabric on the loom.

## Stage 4 — Coating / lamination

**What it is and when it happens in sequence.** This is the clearest sequencing statement
on the site:

> "A coated bag is constructed like a standard bag, however, **before it is sewn
> together** an additional polypropylene film is added to the bag's fabric sealing the
> small gaps in the weaves."
> — [FIBC Vocabulary](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/a-guide-to-flexible-intermediate-bulk-container-vocabulary)
> (same wording in [Coated vs Uncoated](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/how-to-choose-between-coated-and-uncoated-fibc-bulk-bags-for-your-application))

So: **coating is applied to the fabric before cutting/sewing, not to the finished bag.**

**Which side.** "The film can be added to the inside or outside of the bag." Inside is
"most common" because it "can keep products like powders from getting stuck in the weave
when discharged"
([Coated vs Uncoated](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/how-to-choose-between-coated-and-uncoated-fibc-bulk-bags-for-your-application)).
Another article says bags "are coated with a polypropylene laminate which can be on the
inside of the bag, the outside of the bag, or both"
([Watertight or Waterproof?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-construction/are-bulk-bags-watertight-or-waterproof)).

**Glossary definition.** "Coated Fabric/Bags — Fabric or bag which is coated/laminated
with polypropylene (PP) to prevent moisture intrusion or sifting of the contents"
([FIBC Bulk Bag Glossary](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/)).

**Explicit performance limits.** "This polypropylene laminate does not make the FIBC
waterproof or watertight which is a common misconception." It "can act as an adequate
moisture barrier and can significantly reduce fine material leakage"
([Watertight or Waterproof?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-construction/are-bulk-bags-watertight-or-waterproof)).
"Coating only acts as a moisture barrier. Coating will not keep liquid out, and it will
not keep liquid in"
([Coated vs Uncoated](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/how-to-choose-between-coated-and-uncoated-fibc-bulk-bags-for-your-application)).

**Field test for coated vs uncoated:** "press the weave together to see if it spreads
apart" — coated fabric will not spread, uncoated will
([Coated vs Uncoated](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/how-to-choose-between-coated-and-uncoated-fibc-bulk-bags-for-your-application)).

**Coating vs. sift-resistant seams as alternatives:** "coated bags are the most
cost-effective option and work for most products"
([Sift Resistant Seams](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/specialty-bulk-bags-fibc-big-bags-with-sift-resistant-seams)).

**GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com** — the extrusion-coating machine/process
itself; coating thickness or coating weight (GSM/microns); coating line speed; adhesion
or peel testing; corona treatment.

## Stage 6 — Printing

**What can be printed.** Customization options are "custom printed color logos, warnings
and/or hazard labels, stripes or other patterns (including color patterns)"
([Ordering FIBCs with Custom Aesthetics](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/all-about-ordering-fibcs-custom-aesthetics)).
Custom printing "increases brand visibility" and is "especially useful if you're using
multiple types of FIBCs in your facility and need to differentiate between them," and
"particularly useful if you're using bulk bags with chemicals and/or hazardous materials."
Custom printing is grouped with "colored loops, document pouches, custom printing, and
sift-proof stitching" as small changes that improve safety and efficiency
([How to Make Small Changes for Maximum Impact](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bags/how-to-make-small-changes-for-maximum-impact-in-bulk-bags)).
Custom printing is also listed as a spec option for WPP bags
([Woven Polypropylene Bags](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/produce-packaging/woven-polypropylene-bags/)).

**Commercial reality of printed orders.** Sourcing custom-printed bags requires
distributors to maintain "close relationships" with overseas manufacturers and "good
communication throughout the process"; custom bags "generally have long lead times"
([Ordering FIBCs with Custom Aesthetics](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/all-about-ordering-fibcs-custom-aesthetics)).
Pre-pandemic, custom bag orders ran "about 90 days lead time"
([Lead Times](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/what-impacts-fibc-jumbo-bag-lead-times)).

**GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com** — printing process (flexographic, screen,
etc.); ink type or food-safe ink requirements; number of colors available; number of
printable sides/panels; plate or setup charges; minimum order quantity for printing;
whether printing occurs on fabric roll before cutting or on assembled panels.

## Stage 7 — Sewing / stitching and body construction

### 7a. Body construction types (how many pieces get sewn)

| Type | How it is assembled | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **U-Panel** | "constructed with three body panels – one that runs the full length of the bag forming the bottom and two sides and two additional pieces of fabric sewn into the larger piece that form the other two sides"; also written as "3 body panels (often referred to as U+2 to reflect the single piece of material sewn together with the 2 side panels)" | [Types of Jumbo Bag Construction](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/types-of-jumbo-bag-construction-upanel-circular-4panel), [Different Types of FIBC Construction](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/what-are-the-different-types-fibc-construction) |
| **Circular / Tubular** | "made with a tubular piece of fabric that forms all sides of the bag allowing for stitching only for the top and bottom" | [Types of Jumbo Bag Construction](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/types-of-jumbo-bag-construction-upanel-circular-4panel) |
| **4-Panel** | "constructed with four individual pieces sewn together to form a square structure." Drawback: "the large amount of stitching required to keep the bag together," which "can allow for more areas of the bag prone to failure." Benefit: they "hold their square shape better than other types of constructions due to the way they're put together." Noted as uncommon today | [Types of Jumbo Bag Construction](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/types-of-jumbo-bag-construction-upanel-circular-4panel), [Different Types of FIBC Construction](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/what-are-the-different-types-fibc-construction) |
| **Baffle** | "extra internal sewn-in fabric panels into each of the bag's four corners"; also described as "extra fabric panels **or pieces of string** sewn into each of the bag's four corners" to hold square shape during filling | [Baffle Bags Transformed](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/baffle-bags-transformed), [Types of Jumbo Bag Construction](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/types-of-jumbo-bag-construction-upanel-circular-4panel) |

The glossary/site definition of the term: bag construction "refers to the way that sheets
of woven polypropylene are sewn/stitched together to form a bag."

### 7b. The needle-hole problem — the central sewing constraint the site returns to

> "During the manufacturing process, sewing machine needles make small holes in the
> fabric of the bag. These can cause a leak if the bag is filled with very fine
> products/materials."
> — [Sift Resistant Seams](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/specialty-bulk-bags-fibc-big-bags-with-sift-resistant-seams)

Every sift-proofing technique on the site is a response to this.

### 7c. Sift-resistant / sift-proof seam construction

- Definition: "Sift proof seams are seams in a bulk bag that are specifically designed to
  prevent fine materials (generally powders) from leaking through the seams"
  ([FIBC Vocabulary](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/a-guide-to-flexible-intermediate-bulk-container-vocabulary)).
- Method: "using a felt or spongy type yarn in the sewing process." The operator "feeds
  the sift cord along the seam of the bag when sewing, filling the areas near the needle
  hole."
- Felt placement: felt may be "sewn either along the threaded seam or in between the
  fabric panels."
- **Most robust configuration stated:** "one strip of felt between the polypropylene seam
  with filler cord on the top and bottom of the seam."
  ([Sift Resistant Seams](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/specialty-bulk-bags-fibc-big-bags-with-sift-resistant-seams))
- Filler cord definition: "Typically a polypropylene material used in manufacturing
  sift-resistant FIBC'S. A rope or braided yarn-like cord that is sewn into a seam to help
  prevent the escape of fine dusts and powders"
  ([FIBC Bulk Bag Glossary](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/)).
- Sift-resistant construction "typically utilizes coated fabric and filler cord in the sew
  lines."

### 7d. Stitchless / heat-fused construction (Fusion bags)

An alternative to sewing entirely, in the containment area:
- "heat-sealed seams in the product containment area, eliminating stitching where it
  matters most."
- "Heat-bonded seams work differently retaining significantly more of the original fabric
  strength by **fusing fibers rather than piercing them**."
- Result: "100% sift-proof bulk bags with no stitch holes" and "A cleaner interior with no
  loose threads or residual fibers."
- Process control: "All Fusion bulk bags are produced using the same licensed process and
  specifications, resulting in predictable performance across manufacturers and production
  runs."
([Why Fusion Bulk Bags Are Built Differently](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/why-fusion-bulk-bags-are-built-differently))

### 7e. Stitch types, thread, seam engineering

`GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com`

Despite covering seams extensively from a *performance* angle, the site never names a
stitch class. **Chain stitch, lock stitch, and safety stitch do not appear anywhere on
nationalbulkbag.com or blog.nationalbulkbag.com.** Also absent: sewing thread material or
denier/tex, stitches per inch, needle size, seam classes (superimposed / lapped / bound),
single vs. double row stitching, seam allowance, number of sewing operators or machine
types.

## Stage 8 — Lift loop attachment

**Definition.** "Lift loops are fabric loops attached to each of the four corners at the
top of the bag"
([FIBC Vocabulary](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/a-guide-to-flexible-intermediate-bulk-container-vocabulary)).

**Loop types and how they are attached:**
- **Loop over Loop (corner seam loops):** "Each corner has a lift loop **sewn into the
  seam of the bag**." All four loops must be used to lift. "The most commonly used type of
  lift loop construction."
- **Cross Corner Loops:** loops "sewn into eight points in opposite corners of the bag."
  They "require that the bag is made from circular fabric." Designed so "lifting loops
  stand upright, making it easier for forklift operators."
- **Stevedore Straps** — three variants: *Single* — "a loop pulled through the four
  standard corner loops to connect all"; *Double* — "two loops that connect each parallel
  loop at the corners"; *Supporting* — "supporting loops that are attached to each corner
  loop." These enable lifting "from only one or two" points.
- **Sleeve / Lift Tunnel Loops:** "Made from bulk bag body fabric," "can be designed to be
  at the bottom or the top of the bag," and are "only usable by forklifts."
([Types of Lift Loops](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/fibc-bulk-bag-101-types-of-lift-loops-explained))

**GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com** — loop webbing material (polyester vs. nylon
vs. polypropylene webbing) is never specified; webbing width or tensile rating; the
stitch pattern used to attach loops (e.g. box-X / bartack); number of stitch rows; loop
height tolerances; loop colour-coding standards.

## Stage 9 — Liner insertion

**Attachment options (five, verbatim from the source):**
1. **Loose Drop-In** — "A liner inserted into the bag without being attached."
2. **Tabbed (Tacked)** — "The liner is lightly sewn or glued to interior seams using small
   attachment tabs."
3. **Sewn-In** — "The bulk bag liner is stitched directly into the bag construction, often
   around seams or the spout."
4. **Form-Fit / Structured** — "A liner designed to match the internal geometry of the
   bag," integrated with filling equipment, sometimes using "flanged tops."
5. **Glue-Attached** — "The liner is adhered to the interior of the bag using adhesive
   bonding."
([Comparing Liner Attachment Options](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/comparing-different-liner-attachment-options-for-bulk-bag-applications))

Liners are referenced elsewhere as **polyethylene liners** used as a moisture/protective
layer where coating alone is insufficient
([Watertight or Waterproof?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-construction/are-bulk-bags-watertight-or-waterproof)),
and food-grade bags may carry "inner liners as moisture/oxygen barriers"
([What Really Makes a FIBC Food-Grade?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/whatreallymakesafibcfoodgrade)).
The site maintains separate articles on polyethylene liners and polypropylene liners
(see the [Bulk Bag Liners topic](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/topic/bulk-bag-liners)).

**GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com** — how the liner is physically inserted on the
production line; liner gauge/mil thickness; liner manufacture (blown film, gusseting);
liner sealing method; sequencing of liner insertion relative to final closing seam.

## Stage 10 — Quality control and testing

### 10a. Batch numbers and batch testing (the site's core QC concept)

- **Batch numbers:** "Batch numbers (also referred to as 'lot numbers') are designations
  that are printed on the product and/or product batch that allows the history of the
  product to be traced back to the manufacturer." They enable rapid identification and
  recall of defective product from a specific manufacturing run
  ([Quality Control and FIBCs](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/quality-control-fibcs-batch-numbers-testing-tracking)).
- **Batch testing:** "a process carried out by the manufacturer where **individual bags
  are pulled out of each batch** and rigorously tested for quality, durability, and
  overall performance." It "aims to prevent faulty bags from shipping"
  ([Quality Control and FIBCs](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/quality-control-fibcs-batch-numbers-testing-tracking)).
- Manufacturers should have "QC (quality control) processes in place and be **constantly
  batch testing bags as they come off the line**"; "Batch labeling and testing is a key
  part of this process"
  ([Vetting Overseas Suppliers](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/5-things-quality-distributors-do-to-vet-their-overseas-suppliers-manufacturers)).

### 10b. Safety factor designed into the bag

- "Safety Factor — An industry standard requiring the FIBC to handle five or six times its
  Safe Working Load, (SWL), normally written as a ratio, '5:1 or 6:1 SF'"
  ([FIBC Bulk Bag Glossary](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/)).
- "Safe Working Load (SWL) — The amount of load, (in pounds or kilograms), which a bag is
  designed to carry"
  ([FIBC Bulk Bag Glossary](https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/)).
- **5:1 bags** are "manufactured for one use/one trip"; "Most woven polypropylene bulk
  bags are manufactured for one use/one trip." **6:1 bags** are "specifically manufactured
  for multiple uses." Standard cited: **ISO 21898**. For multi-use bags, FIBCA recommends
  random top-lift testing at manufacturer-determined frequencies, inspection for lift
  strap damage/contamination/moisture, and replacement of web ties and labels between uses
  ([5:1 vs 6:1](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/fibc-bulk-bag-safety-51-vs-61)).

### 10c. UN certification test battery

All UN test bags are prepared **filled to at least 95% of capacity**, using the actual
material or a safe substitute of similar weight and texture
([UN Testing Part 1](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/un-certified-fibc-bulk-bags/un-certified-bulk-bags-rigorous-testing-procedures-part-1-of-2);
[All About UN FIBCs](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/un-certified-fibc-bulk-bags/all-about-un-fibc-bulk-bags)).

| Test | Procedure and parameters | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **Vibration** | Bag sits on a vibration table at a frequency high enough to lift the bag slightly. **60 minutes** | "The bag must not rupture or leak" |
| **Top Lift** | Lifted from top and/or side by an approved lifting device, loaded to **six times its rated weight**, held **five minutes** (also stated as "maintain 6:1 load for 5 mins") | "The bag must not lose shape or cannot loose any contents" |
| **Stack** | Heavy load placed on top equal to **1.8 times the total weight of the bags that might be stacked**, for **24 hours** | "The bag must stay intact and not leak" / "no content loss" |
| **Drop** | Dropped on the weakest bottom section from a height set by packaging group: **PG I 70.9 in, PG II 47.3 in, PG III 31.5 in** | "The bag must not lose any contents. A small, quick release of material is allowed" |
| **Topple** | Dropped from the same heights, landing on any top area over a smooth surface | "The bag must keep all its contents inside, with only a brief release allowed" |
| **Righting** | For bags lifted from top or side. Bag starts on its side and is lifted upright at a set speed. **Two-strap bags require one lifting device; four-strap bags require two devices** | "The bag and its lifting straps must not be damaged in a way that makes them unsafe to use" |
| **Tear** | A **45-degree cut halfway between the bottom and the top level of the contents**. Part one: a heavy load (**twice the safe working limit**) placed on the bag for a minimum of **five minutes**. Part two: bag lifted and held in the air for **five minutes** | "The cut must not grow more than **25%** longer than it was at the start" |

Sources: [UN Testing Part 1](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/un-certified-fibc-bulk-bags/un-certified-bulk-bags-rigorous-testing-procedures-part-1-of-2),
[UN Testing Part 2](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/un-certified-fibc-bulk-bags/un-certified-bulk-bags-rigorous-testing-procedures-part-2-of-2),
[All About UN FIBCs](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/un-certified-fibc-bulk-bags/all-about-un-fibc-bulk-bags).

Beyond testing, UN compliance also imposes "Identification, tagging, and labeling
requirements" and "Specifications on max volume"
([All About UN FIBCs](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/un-certified-fibc-bulk-bags/all-about-un-fibc-bulk-bags)).

### 10d. UV exposure limit

> "Our bags at NBB adhere to the **ISO 21898 Standard** which states that bag exposure to
> UV rays should **never exceed 200 hours**."
> — [What is UV Stabilization?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-construction/what-is-uv-stabilization-how-does-it-impact-fibc-bulk-bags)

The article notes solar intensity varies by region so "different elements can have a
significant factor on this time-frame."

### 10e. Visual inspection

"Light table visual inspection for quality assurance" is listed as a food-grade facility
control
([Top 5 Things to Look For](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/top-5-things-to-look-for-when-selecting-a-food-grade-fibc-supplier)).
In the reconditioning/reuse context, a light table is used to reveal "even the smallest
tears or punctures" invisible to the naked eye
([6 Key Steps to Inspecting Sustainable Bulk Bags](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/6-key-steps-to-inspecting-sustainable-bulk-bags)).

### 10f. Facility-level certifications distributors check

"BRC Food Certification (food grade), SQF Certification (food grade), ISO 9001,"
"ISO 14001 (Environmental Management), ISO 18001 (Occupational Health and Safety),
ISO 22000 (Food Safety Management), ISO 27001 (Information Security)," plus WRAP,
Together for Sustainability, and SMETA audits. Distributors also verify suppliers are
"investing in themselves and their facilities" and updating equipment and processes
([Vetting Overseas Suppliers](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/5-things-quality-distributors-do-to-vet-their-overseas-suppliers-manufacturers)).

### 10g. Testing gaps

`GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com` for the following, which the brief asked about:

  explicitly stops at safety-factor ratios and does not give cyclic test cycle counts.

  values, no standard cited.
- **Sample retention** — no retained-sample policy, retention period, or sample library

- **AQL / defect classification** (critical/major/minor), sampling plan size, or number of

## Stage 11 — Cleaning, food-grade and clean-room handling, metal detection

**The site's headline claim:** "The only main difference between a food-grade FIBC and a
standard bulk bag is **where it is manufactured**." Food-grade FIBCs are produced in
"BRC-certified, food-safe facilities, which follow strict hygiene protocols to prevent
contamination." BRC certification "confirms that the facility meets globally recognized
standards for food safety, quality, and hygiene"
([What Really Makes a FIBC Food-Grade?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/whatreallymakesafibcfoodgrade)).

**Clean-room controls, as listed by the site.** Food-grade bags from BRC-certified
facilities are "produced in a clean room that adheres to the highest of standards
including but not limited to":
- "Proper ventilation" for air quality
- "Vacuum air cleaning for optimal air quality, humidity, and temperature"
- "Pest control procedures to prevent all pests from contaminating the FIBCs"
- **"Metal detection to prevent any metals from the manufacturing process from
  contaminating the FIBCs"**
- "Light table visual inspection for quality assurance"
- "Proper personnel wardrobe" and "Proper personnel hygiene requirements"
- "A HACCP program – Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Program"
- "Proper record keeping procedures for quick troubleshooting and diagnostics"
([Food Grade Myths and Facts](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/food-grade-bulk-bags-3-myths-and-facts);
[Top 5 Things to Look For](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/top-5-things-to-look-for-when-selecting-a-food-grade-fibc-supplier))

The same control list is repeated as evidence that overseas plants meet US standards:
"Many bulk bag manufacturers abroad now use the highest industry standards including BRC
and AIB"
([USA vs Overseas](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/fibc-bulk-bags-manufacturing-usa-versus-overseas)).

**Standards named.** BRC (British Retail Consortium) "became a GFSI (Global Food Safety
Initiative) recognized standard in 1998." Certifications listed: **BRC / BRCGS,
SQF Ed 7.2, FSSC 22000 (ISO 22000 with PAS 223), IFS Pack Secure**. The site explicitly
debunks "FDA approved bags" — the FDA does not issue approvals for FIBCs; the resin meets
21 CFR 177.1520
([Food Grade Myths and Facts](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/food-grade-bulk-bags-3-myths-and-facts)).

**Cleanliness designed into construction.** Fusion's stitchless containment area yields "A
cleaner interior with no loose threads or residual fibers," with "all virgin materials and
approved for direct food contact"
([Why Fusion Bulk Bags Are Built Differently](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/why-fusion-bulk-bags-are-built-differently)).
Food-grade bags may also use "specialized stitching or sealing to reduce contamination"
and "static control features for fine powders"
([What Really Makes a FIBC Food-Grade?](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/whatreallymakesafibcfoodgrade)).

**GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com** — clean-room classification (ISO 14644 class,
particle counts); metal detector sensitivity (Fe/non-Fe/SS ball sizes) or whether every
bag or a sample passes through; air pressure differentials; gowning specifics; how
finished food-grade bags are bagged/sealed in poly before leaving the clean room.

## Stage 12 — Baling, packing, and container loading

**Palletization of empty bags:**
> "Before being filled, bags come **125-500 per pallet** depending on the size of the bag."
> — [3 Key Benefits of Switching from Gaylord Boxes to Bulk Bags](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/3-key-benefits-of-switching-from-gaylord-boxes-to-bulk-bags)

The same article notes empty bags arrive **compressed on pallets** before being filled and
require far less space than gaylord boxes. Palletizing "allows for more secure stacking
and makes it easier to fit large quantities of bags in semi-trucks for transport." A
related storage comparison: "up to 200 flexible intermediate bulk containers on one pallet
versus 50 empty gaylords in the same amount of space"
([Transporting and Shipping FIBCs](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/all-about-transporting-shipping-fibcs)).

**Factory-to-port chain:** "When FIBCs are manufactured overseas, they must be shipped
locally to the port, wait to be **packed into a crate**, loaded onto the ship, and then
transported across the ocean to the US. This process can often result in lengthy
lead-times"
([Lead Times](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/what-impacts-fibc-jumbo-bag-lead-times)).

**Lead times.** Pre-pandemic: "around 30-60 days" for overseas standard orders, "about 90
days" for custom. Post-pandemic: "90-120 days or more." Quality distributors target a
"60-90 day lead time" from suppliers. Distributors mitigate with "warehousing networks
across the United States"
([Lead Times](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/what-impacts-fibc-jumbo-bag-lead-times);
[Vetting Overseas Suppliers](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/5-things-quality-distributors-do-to-vet-their-overseas-suppliers-manufacturers)).

**GAP: not covered by nationalbulkbag.com** — baling as a distinct operation (the word
"bale" is not used for empty FIBC packing on the site); bags per bale; bale compression
or strapping; poly wrapping of bales; bags per 20 ft / 40 ft ocean container; container
loading pattern; export documentation.

## Post-production: reconditioning and reuse (adjacent to manufacturing)

Not part of new-bag manufacture, but the site documents a six-step refurbishment line for
EcoTrace™ bags:
1. "Each bag must be vacuumed to remove residual contents."
2. Light table inspection to detect "even the smallest tears or punctures."
3. Repair of damage to "lift loops and duffle tops."
4. A digital system records "each bag's inspection and repair history."
5. Approved bags return to service — lifecycle "has been known to extend up to 20 times."
6. Non-repairable bags are removed from circulation and users notified.
([6 Key Steps to Inspecting Sustainable Bulk Bags](https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/6-key-steps-to-inspecting-sustainable-bulk-bags))
