## Type A FIBC — Definition and Construction

**Source:** https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/types-of-fibc-bulk-bags-a-b-c-d-explained/

- **Construction:** "plain-woven polypropylene and other non-conductive fabrics"
- **Conductive elements:** none
- **Static protection:** none — Type A offers no static protection
- **Grounding:** not applicable

**Safe for:**
- "non-flammable products"

**NOT safe for:**
- "flammable products"
- environments where "flammable solvents or gases" are present
- environments containing a flammable atmosphere with **minimum ignition energy ≤ 1,000 mJ**

**Key numeric threshold:** 1,000 mJ — Type A must not be used where a flammable
atmosphere with MIE at or below this value exists.

## Type B FIBC — Definition and Construction

**Sources:**
- https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/types-of-fibc-bulk-bags-a-b-c-d-explained/
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/static-electricity-in-fibcs-how-to-avoid-the-dangers-of-storing-combustible-products

- **Construction:** "plain-woven polypropylene fabrics or other non-conductive material"
- **Conductive elements:** none — Type B is non-conductive, same as Type A
- **Distinguishing property:** **low breakdown voltage**
- **Numeric breakdown voltage value:** *Not stated on source.* The property is
  named; no volts/kV figure is published.
- **Mechanism:** low breakdown voltage prevents the formation of **propagating
  brush discharges (PBD)**
- **Grounding:** not applicable / not required

**Safe for:**
- "dry, flammable powders"
- "dust with ignition energies less than 3mJ"

**NOT safe for:**
- environments where "flammable solvents or gases" are present

**Key numeric threshold:** < 3 mJ — the dust ignition-energy environment Type B
is intended for.

> Type B is the only type distinguished purely by an electrical *property* of an
> otherwise non-conductive fabric, not by added conductive material.

## Type C FIBC — Definition and Construction

**Sources:**
- https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/types-of-fibc-bulk-bags-a-b-c-d-explained/
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/static-electricity-in-fibcs-how-to-avoid-the-dangers-of-storing-combustible-products
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/the-increased-risks-associated-with-type-c-fibc-big-bags

- **Construction:** "non-conductive polypropylene fabrics interwoven with
  conducting threads traditionally sewn in a grid pattern"
- **Thread arrangement:** grid pattern; threads "must be electrically
  interconnected"
- **Grounding:** threads must be "connected to a designated ground/earth during
  filling and discharging"
- **Also called:** conductive FIBCs (per source usage)

**Safe for:**
- "flammable powders"
- environments with "flammable vapors, gases, or combustable dusts" *(source spelling)*

**NOT safe / protection void when:**
- "ground connection is not present or has become damaged"

## Type D FIBC — Definition and Construction

**Sources:**
- https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/types-of-fibc-bulk-bags-a-b-c-d-explained/
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/static-electricity-in-fibcs-how-to-avoid-the-dangers-of-storing-combustible-products

- **Construction:** "antistatic and static dissipative fabrics"
- **Yarn:** contains "quasi-conductive yarns that safely dissipate static
  electricity into the atmosphere via safe, low-energy **corona discharge**"
- **Interconnection:** quasi-conductive yarns are **not** interconnected
  (contrast with Type C grid)
- **Grounding:** **not required**

**Prevents (all three named explicitly):**
- incendiary sparks
- brush discharges
- propagating brush discharges

**Safe for:**
- "flammable powders"
- environments with "flammable vapors, gases, or combustable dusts" *(source spelling)*

**NOT safe / protection void when:**
- bag "surface contaminated or coated with conductive material such as grease"

## Type Selection — Consolidated Comparison

**Sources:** types-of-fibc-bulk-bags page + static-electricity blog article.

> **No source page publishes a formal decision matrix.** The table below is
> assembled strictly from the per-type safe/unsafe statements quoted in the
> sections above. Every cell traces to a source statement; the *tabular
> arrangement* is this document's, not the source's.

| | Type A | Type B | Type C | Type D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fabric | Plain-woven PP, non-conductive | Plain-woven PP, non-conductive | Non-conductive PP + conducting threads | Antistatic / static-dissipative |
| Conductive element | None | None | Conducting threads, grid pattern, interconnected | Quasi-conductive yarns, NOT interconnected |
| Distinguishing property | — | Low breakdown voltage | Interconnected conductive grid | Corona discharge dissipation |
| Grounding required | N/A | N/A | **Yes** — during filling and discharging | **No** |
| Non-flammable product | Safe | Safe | Safe | Safe |
| Flammable / dry flammable powder | **Not safe** | Safe (dust MIE < 3 mJ) | Safe | Safe |
| Flammable solvents / gases / vapors in atmosphere | **Not safe** | **Not safe** | Safe | Safe |
| Combustible dust atmosphere | **Not safe** (MIE ≤ 1,000 mJ) | Safe (< 3 mJ dust) | Safe | Safe |
| Fails when | Any flammability present | Solvents/gases present | Ground absent or damaged | Surface coated with conductive material e.g. grease |

**Decision logic derivable from the sources:**

1. Product non-flammable AND no flammable atmosphere → Type A acceptable.
2. Product is a dry flammable powder (dust MIE < 3 mJ) AND no flammable
   solvents/gases in atmosphere → Type B.
3. Flammable solvents, gases, or vapors present in the atmosphere → **Type C or
   Type D only.** Both A and B are explicitly excluded.
4. Between C and D: Type C requires a correct ground connection every cycle;
   Type D requires no ground but requires an uncontaminated surface.

`UNVERIFIED:` The four-quadrant framing (flammable product? × flammable
atmosphere?) is a synthesis of the source's narrative statements. National Bulk
Bag does not present it as a matrix, and does not state a rule for "non-flammable
product in a flammable atmosphere" as a distinct case.

## Static Electricity and Discharge Types

**Source:** https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/static-electricity-in-fibcs-how-to-avoid-the-dangers-of-storing-combustible-products

**Generation mechanism:**
> "When bulk bags are filled and discharged, the movement of finely powdered
> substances can cause a buildup of static electricity."

**Discharge types named across the sources:**

| Discharge type | What the source says | Which type addresses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Propagating brush discharge (PBD)** | "highly energetic and dangerous" | Type B (via low breakdown voltage); Type D |
| **Brush discharge** | Named as a hazard to be prevented | Type D |
| **Incendiary spark / spark discharge** | Named as a hazard to be prevented | Type D |
| **Corona discharge** | "safe, low-energy" — the *desired* dissipation mechanism, not a hazard | Type D mechanism |

**Cone discharge / bulking brush discharge:** *Not stated on any source page.*

**Ignition energy figures published:**

| Value | Context |
|---|---|
| ≤ 1,000 mJ | Flammable atmosphere MIE above which Type A is disallowed |
| < 3 mJ | Dust ignition energy environment Type B is intended for |

No kV, volt, or ohm figures appear on any source page.

## Type C Grounding — Requirements and Failure Modes

**Source:** https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/the-increased-risks-associated-with-type-c-fibc-big-bags

**Requirement:**
- A **cable attached to the sidewall** "must be grounded in order to function properly"
- When correctly grounded the cable "transfers any static … to the cable and
  eventually into the ground"
- Grounding is required **during filling and discharging**
- Described as: "A proper connection to the designated ground during filling and
  discharging is imperative."

**Failure modes and risk:**
- If grounding is forgotten or improper, the bag's anti-static properties become
  "completely worthless"
- Allows "static to build up in the bag unbeknownst to those handling it"
- Consequence: risk of "injury or in situations where volatile materials are
  involved, death"

**Type C vs Type D risk comparison (source's own framing):**

| Aspect | Type C | Type D |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Requires cable grounding | Specialized fabric/coating |
| Human-error exposure | High — requires a correct user action every cycle | Significantly reduced |
| Grounding needed | Yes | No |

**Source recommendation:** Type D is preferred, on the basis of "significantly
reducing the human element."

**Not stated:** ground resistance value in ohms, grounding cable specification,
continuity-test procedure, test frequency, IEC 61340-4-4.

## Safety Factor — 5:1 vs 6:1

**Sources:**
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/fibc-bulk-bag-safety-51-vs-61
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-safety/5-1-6-1-and-purchasing-used-fibcs

**Term used by source:** "Safety Factor Ratio (SFR)". The source does not give a
mathematical definition of safety factor.

| Rating | Meaning | Trip class | Reuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5:1** | Bag will "hold five times the amount of their safe work load (SWL)" / "hold 5 times the rated bag weight" | Single use / single trip | **Prohibited.** "Never use a 5:1 safety ratio bag for more than ONE use/trip" |
| **6:1** | Bag will "hold six times their rated safe working load" / "hold 6 times the rated capacity" | Multiple use / multi trip | Permitted, subject to FIBCA reuse protocol |

**6:1 reuse protocol (per FIBCA guidelines, as cited by source):**
- cleaning
- reconditioning
- inspection
- tracking
- random top-lift testing — frequency "determined by manufacturer and/or user"

**Purchasing used FIBCs:**
- Obtain "documentation from the seller that details their inspection and
  certification process"
- Do not exceed rated capacity weights — described as "extremely dangerous"

**Not stated:** test cycle counts, specific test load values, breaking-strength
or ultimate-strength terminology, 8:1 ratio, percentage retention criteria,
damage criteria that disqualify a bag from reuse.

`UNVERIFIED:` The source never uses the phrase "breaking strength" or "ultimate
strength". It expresses the ratio as a multiple of SWL only.

## Safe Working Load (SWL)

**Sources:**
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/how-to-weigh-industrial-fibc-bulk-bags-safe-working-load
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/fibc-bulk-bag-safety-51-vs-61
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/how-to-find-a-safe-fibc-for-your-product

**Definitions given:**
- "The safe working load of a bag is the safe rated amount of weight (expressed
  in pounds in the United States) that a bulk bag can effectively hold without
  failing."
- "Maximum load limit that can be safely carried by a bulk bag without risking
  damage or injury"

**Consequence of exceeding SWL:**
> "Bag failure is extremely dangerous and can lead to injury and/or death."

**Published capacity figures:**

| Figure | Context |
|---|---|
| 2,200 – 3,000 lbs | Most common SWL range |
| up to 4,000 lbs | Custom bag capacity |
| up to 5,000 lbs | Weight a bag may reach, cited in handling guidance |

**Worked example given by source (bag sizing/weight calculation):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bag dimensions | 35" x 35" x 40" |
| Volume | 34.1 cubic feet |
| Product density (almonds) | 30 lbs/ft³ |
| Resulting total weight | 989.6 lbs |

**Weighing methods named:**
- Pallet wrappers with built-in scales at their bases
- Specialized filling equipment with weighing capability (during or after filling)
- Industrial pallet scales — capacity 5,000–10,000+ lbs, cost under $3,000

**Not stated:** tare weight handling, relation of SWL to breaking strength.

## The 7 Critical Points for Filling and Emptying

**Source:** https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/7-critical-points-filling-emptying-bulk-bag-fibc

1. **Always Use Every Loop** — loops "use in tandem with each other to lift the
   full rated weight."
2. **Never Gather Loops** — avoid lifting with a single hook unless the bag is
   specifically designed for it.
3. **Keep Loops Vertical** — loops are designed for vertical lifting, not
   horizontal / side-to-side loading.
4. **Keep Clear of Suspended FIBCs** — never stand underneath or close to a
   suspended bag.
5. **Never Exceed Safe Working Load (SWL)** — rated capacity must not be
   exceeded under any circumstances.
6. **Fill Bag Evenly and Keep Stable** — fill, store, and empty in a stable
   manner to prevent toppling.
7. **Close Bag as Instructed** — proper closure prevents leakage during storage
   and transport.

**Additional points from the same article:**
- Verify the bottom discharge spout is "closed securely and properly before filling"
- Type C and Type D bags are recommended for flammable/explosive materials
- Handling equipment must be "rated to handle the rated weight"
- Safety latches "NEED to be used at ALL TIMES"

## Safe Filling Practices

**Source:** https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/safe-fibc-handling-guidelines-filling-and-discharging

Verify before and during filling that:
- The bag is "free from damage that can compromise its strength"
- The "bottom discharge option is properly closed and secure"
- The bag is "filled evenly"
- The "filled bag remains stable at all times"
- The "bag's fill spout is closed properly when the filling is complete"
- "All of the bags lift loops or sleeves are vertical"
- The "bag is raised and lowered smoothly"
- "Electrostatic concerns are addressed in flammable or explosive environments"

**Not stated:** loop tension values, ordered step sequence, fill-rate limits,
spout clamp specification.

## Forklift, Crane, and Hoist Handling

**Sources:**
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/safe-fibc-handling-guidelines-cranes-hoists-forklifts
- https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/revisiting-fibc-bulk-bag-safety-forklifts-cranes-hoists

**Forklift rules:**
- "The distance between the forklift tines is the correct width for the bag"
- Tines must not have sharp edges that damage lift loops
- **"Rounded edges with a radius of 5mm at minimum are preferred"** — the only
  dimensional spec published across all pages
- "The forklift operator holds the bag close to the mast as low as possible,
  tilted back to a proper angle"
- Never tilt the mast forward when handling bags
- "The forklift is stopped before raising or lowering the bag"
- Prohibited: "Lower or raise a bag when the forklift is moving"
- "Avoid fast starts and stops"

**Crane and hoist rules:**
- Equipment must be "specifically designed" for bag handling
- Hooks/bars must lack sharp edges to prevent lift-loop damage
- "Ensure safety latches exist and are being used"
- Never gather loops to lift with a single hook — exception only where the bag is
  specifically designed for single-loop lifting

**Lift loop rules:**
- "All lift loops/sleeves are vertical to prevent damage"
- Prohibited: "Lift a bag with fewer lift loops or sleeves than provided"
- "The bag is raised and lowered smoothly"

**Personnel:**
- Prohibited: "Allow personnel under a suspended bag"
- "Never under any circumstances allow a person to be underneath a suspended bag"
- "All personnel wear the proper safety gear and stay clear of any potential hazards"
- "All personnel are clear before lifting or lowering a bag"

**Not stated:** tine length requirement, tine covering/padding requirement,
maximum lift height, tilt angle in degrees, loop angle in degrees, travel speed
limits.

## Storing Bulk Bags

**Source:** https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/safe-fibc-handling-guidelines-storing-empty-full-bulk-bags

**Storing FULL bags — two stacking methods named:**

| Method | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Pyramid stacking** | "each bag above the first layer sitting on at least four lower bags" |
| **Supported stacking** | stacking bags against two retaining walls of adequate strength |

**Pallet rule:** bags "must not hang over pallet sides unless specifically
approved by manufacturer/supplier"; examine pallets for appropriate sizing to
avoid overhang.

**Storing EMPTY bags:**
- Store in a "safe and clean facility that protects them from UV rays and moisture"
- "Cover your bags to prevent them from being exposed to dirt, dust, and moisture"
- Bags must be "free from any damage"

**UV exposure:**
- "Direct UV rays will weaken the strength of the bag over time"
- **No exposure-hour limit or degradation percentage is published.**

**Not stated:** maximum stack height, temperature limits, humidity limits,
indoor/outdoor rules beyond avoiding direct sunlight and inclement weather, pest
control, sharp-object clearance specification.
