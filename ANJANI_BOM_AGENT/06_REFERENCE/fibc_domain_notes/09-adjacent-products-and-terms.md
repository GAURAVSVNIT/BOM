# Adjacent Products, Commercial Terms & Pharmaceutical Requirements

## Reading note on source quality

The **produce packaging** pages are marketing/category pages, not spec sheets. Across all nine, the
site repeatedly defers hard specs to a gated "Download In-Stock List" PDF and a phone number. Concrete
numbers exist only for burlap, netting, V-Board, and Gaylord dimensions. Everything else is
qualitative. Each product below therefore ends with an explicit **Not stated** list so gaps are visible
rather than silently absent.

By contrast the **return policy** and the **pharmaceutical blog articles** are dense and quotable, and
are the most commercially useful material in this file.

## Woven polypropylene bags (WPP) — the direct analogue to the parent sack line

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/produce-packaging/woven-polypropylene-bags/

### What it is

> "Typically smaller than FIBCs, woven polypropylene agricultural bags still offer great benefits."

Described as "lightweight, strong, and breathable packaging materials designed for carrying a variety of
agricultural materials."

### How it differs from an FIBC

The only stated differentiator is **size** — WPP bags are "typically smaller than FIBCs." The page draws
no distinction on lift loops, safety factor, SWL, discharge, or handling method. WPP bags are described
in terms of **weight class** (25lb, 50lb) where FIBCs are described in terms of **SWL** (2,200–3,000 lbs).

### Stated advantages (verbatim, four bullets)

- Lightweight and strong material
- Can be fully custom printed on
- Naturally breathable
- Fully Customizable to fit filling and/or discharge equipment

### Customization options

The page gives these as a single prose sentence, **not** a spec table:

> "Options include the size of the bag, fabric weight (GSM), bag construction, seam style, top
> construction, and custom printing."

So the **six customization axes** are: size, fabric weight (GSM), bag construction, seam style, top
construction, custom printing.

### Venting

> "Some WPP bags have additional venting to allow maximum breathability."

### Printing

> "can accommodate custom prints in high quality (however, not photo quality like BOPP)."

This is a meaningful distinction the site draws consistently: **WPP printing is high quality but not
photo quality; BOPP and poly bags are photo quality.**

### Typical uses

Grain, feed, seeds, beans.

### Sizes and capacity

Only **25 lb and 50 lb** weight ratings are referenced.

### In-stock product shown

One product name only: **"WPP White Bag Plastic Collar"** — no SKU given.

### Page headings

"WOVEN POLYPROPYLENE BAGS", "Woven Polypropylene Bags for Your Application", "Woven Polypropylene Bag
Advantages Stock and Custom", "WOVEN POLYPROPYLENE BAGS OFFERING".

### Not stated — flagged because this is the deep-dive target

Despite being the page the parent project cares most about, the following are **all absent**:

- **GSM values** — GSM is named as a customizable axis, but **no actual number is given anywhere**
- **Weave type or mesh count** — not stated
- **Lamination / coating options** — not stated
- **Closure type** (sewn, heat-cut, hemmed, drawstring, sonic) — not stated; "seam style" and "top
  construction" are named as options but never enumerated
- **Bottom construction** — not stated
- **Dimensions in inches** — not stated for any size
- **Number of print colors or print method** — not stated
- **Colors available** — not stated
- **Minimum order quantity** — not stated
- **Lead time** — not stated
- **SKUs / item numbers** — not stated

`UNVERIFIED:` The WPP page is the least specified of the adjacent products. Any claim about NBB's WPP
fabric weights, closures, or MOQs cannot be sourced from this page.

## Mesh produce bags

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/produce-packaging/mesh-produce-bag/

### Two weave constructions — the key technical content on this page

**Leno weave**

> "more stable (as in, they stretch less than other weaves), are 'L sewn', and woven on looms"

**Raschel weave**

> "softer than Leno weaves, are L sewn or tubular, and are made on machines designed for knitting"

So: Leno is **loom-woven and lower-stretch**; Raschel is **knitted, softer, available L-sewn or tubular**.

### In-stock items

|---|---|
| M21x31.5PD | Purple Mesh Bag |
| M22X36G | Green Mesh Bag |
| M10_RO | Red Onion Mesh Bag |

`UNVERIFIED:` The item numbers appear to encode dimensions (M21x31.5PD → 21" x 31.5"; M22X36G →
22" x 36"). The page does not state this convention — pattern observed, not a stated rule.

### Typical uses

Onions ("the most common industry application"), oranges, firewood, corncob, seafood.

### Features

Bags "allow products to breathe freely"; suitable for both retail and processing facility applications.
Customization includes printing and multiple colors.

**Not stated:** weight capacities, material composition, closure types, MOQ, full size list.

## Custom poly bags

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/produce-packaging/custom-poly-bags/

### Material

**Polyethylene** — "a strong, durable material which makes it ideal for many agricultural applications."

### Two types

**Consumer grade poly bags**
- Weight classes: "3lb., 5lb., 8lb., 10lb., etc."
- Vent holes for product breathability
- Support "photo-quality custom prints"

**Baler poly bags**
- "Larger 50lb. balers designed to hold smaller consumer-ready poly bags"
- Also support "photo-quality prints and have vent holes"
- Generic versions lack the printed checkboxes found on paper alternatives

### Customization

> "Photo-quality custom prints are available"

> "Construction fully customized (including the top construction) to accommodate the filling equipment"

> "Position of the vent holes can also be customized during manufacturing"

> "Multiple color options are in-stock and we colors with custom orders" *(sic — quoted as written)*

### How it differs from an FIBC and from WPP

Poly (extruded film) vs woven PP. **Photo-quality printing** is the stated advantage over WPP. Retail
consumer scale (3–10 lb) plus a 50 lb baler tier that aggregates consumer units — a two-tier
retail/distribution structure FIBCs do not have.

**Not stated:** gauge/mil thickness, BOPP options, closure types, MOQ, lead times, item numbers, LDPE vs
HDPE.

## Gaylord boxes

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/produce-packaging/gaylord-box/

### What it is

> "Commonly referred to as pallet boxes, bulk boxes, or skid boxes, Gaylord boxes are pallet sized boxes
> used for storage and shipping of bulk materials and quantities."

The term "Gaylord" specifically denotes **triple-wall corrugated construction**, originating from the
Gaylord Container Company of St. Louis, Missouri.

### Specifications

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Footprint | 40" x 48" (standard pallet size) |
| Heights | 36" or 48" |
| Wall options | Single, double, or triple wall |
| Shipping | Sold and shipped flat |
| Bundle format | 5/150 bundle/bale |

### Typical uses

Agricultural storage and transportation; farm/processing facility bulk storage; retail product display.

### How it differs from an FIBC

Rigid corrugated vs flexible woven. Same pallet footprint (40"x48" vs the FIBC 35" cube at 1 cu yd), but
box-handled rather than crane/forklift-lifted by loops. Ships flat.

**Not stated:** weight capacity in lbs, corrugated grade/flute type, bottom style, lids, vented options,
new vs used grading (A/B), SKUs, MOQ beyond bundle size.

`UNVERIFIED:` The "5/150 bundle/bale" notation is reproduced as written; the page does not explain it.

# Commercial terms

## Return policy and limited warranty

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/return-policy/

Page headings: **"Return Policy"** and **"Return Policy; Limited Warranty"**.

### Legal entity — note this

The policy is written in the name of **Rapid Packaging**, not National Bulk Bag. (Per the sourcing page
captured in `05-industries-and-sourcing.md`, National Bulk Bag is an affiliate of Rapid Packaging.) All
obligations, remedies, and discretion in the policy run to Rapid Packaging.

### Defect / claim window — 30 days

> "All claims regarding product shortage, defects or damage must be made within 30 days after receipt of
> product."

Failure to notify within this window means irrevocable acceptance of the product.

### Inspection obligation on delivery

> "Customers must promptly inspect products upon their arrival, noting any defects, damage or
> discrepancy in quantity."

### RMA process

> "All returns must have a return merchandise authorization (RMA) number issued by Rapid Packaging to be
> accepted for return."

Claims are initiated by contacting customer service at **763-404-8900**.

### Who pays freight

> "Customers are responsible for all shipping charges unless the product is defective or damaged, in
> which case Rapid Packaging will reimburse Customer for shipping charges incurred with documentation."

So: **customer pays by default; reimbursed only for defective/damaged goods, and only with documentation.**

### Warranty

> "Rapid Packaging warrants that, at delivery, products will be free from defects in material and
> workmanship and will meet the specifications agreed to in writing by Rapid Packaging."

Subject to standard industry tolerances.

### Remedies — exclusive and at supplier's discretion

> "Customer's sole and exclusive remedy...is limited to Rapid Packaging's (i) supply of additional
> product to address a shortage; (ii) replacement of the printed products determined by Rapid Packaging
> to be defective; or (iii) refund of the purchase price."

Note the determination of defectiveness sits with Rapid Packaging.

### Limitation of liability

> "Rapid Packaging's liability related to sales of products will be limited to the selling price of any
> products determined by Rapid Packaging to be defective and will in no event include special, indirect,
> incidental, or consequential damages, including lost profits or loss of Customer's clients."

### Quantity tolerance on printed orders — 10%

> "Printing is performed roll to roll, and Rapid Packaging cannot guarantee exact quantities. Production
> over-runs or under-runs not to exceed 10% on quantities ordered will constitute satisfactory
> quantities."

Commercially significant: a printed order may ship **±10%** of the ordered quantity and that is
contractually satisfactory.

### What the return policy does NOT state

Specifically checked and **absent**:

- **Restocking fee** — no percentage, no mention anywhere
- **Custom / made-to-order exclusion** — no clause excluding custom or printed goods from return
- **Condition requirements** for returned goods (resalable, original packaging, unused)
- **Carrier inspection mandate** or requirement to note damage on the delivery receipt/BOL
- **Refund timeline** or refund-vs-credit election rules
- **Return window** distinct from the 30-day claim window

`UNVERIFIED:` The brief asked for restocking fees and custom-order exclusions specifically. Neither
exists in this policy. The absence of a custom-order exclusion is notable given the site sells
extensively custom-printed goods — do not assert one.

## Article: Choosing a Pharmaceutical Grade FIBC Bulk Bag Supplier

Subheadings in order: **BRC Facilities are Key** → **The Importance of FIBC Liners** → **The Dangers of
Static Electricity** → **Thanks for reading!**

### Certifications a supplier should hold

- **ISO 9001:2008** — quality management systems
- **ISO 22000:2005** — food safety management
- **AIB International** — food safety auditing standards
- **BRC Global Standards** meeting **GFSI** (Global Food Safety Initiative) criteria

### BRC manufacturing environment requirements — the eight stated items

The article states BRC standards mandate:

1. Proper ventilation
2. Pest control procedures
3. Metal detection
4. Light table inspection
5. Proper personnel wardrobe
6. Proper personnel hygiene
7. HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) Program
8. Record keeping

### Resin requirement — the single most specific technical claim

> "100% virgin resin is one of the most critical components of a pharmaceutical bag. Standard bags often
> use recycled resin during the bag coating process. This can result in contaminates in the bag's
> coating making it unfit for use in the pharmaceutical industry."

This is the clearest pharma-vs-standard differentiator on the site: **virgin resin in the coating, not
recycled**.

### Liner requirements

Polyethylene liners provide:
- "Extra layer of protection for materials"
- "Keeps outside moisture and contaminates out"
- "Provides better containment for powders"

Supply options: liners "purchased separately and inserted manually into each bag, or they can come
pre-inserted into each bag for ease of handling."

Buyer guidance, quoted: "Remember to ask your supplier about the availability of poly liners when
purchasing your bag."

### Static electricity / FIBC types

> "There are four specific types of bags (A, B, C, and D) each with unique features and safe uses. Types
> B-D are specifically equipped with features that help mitigate the build up of static."

Buyer guidance: "be sure to have a conversation about what type is best for your application."

**Note:** this is the **only** place across everything scraped so far where FIBC Types A/B/C/D are named
at all — they are absent from every product page including Chemicals & Pharmaceuticals.

## Article: Dangerous Chemicals, Pharmaceuticals, and FIBCs

Subheadings in order: **Pharmaceutical Applications and Food Grade FIBCs** → **Dangerous Materials and UN
Certified FIBCs** → **High Temperature Products**.

### Opening framing (verbatim)

> "We've covered a large number of topics on the NBB blog over the last year, but one area we haven't
> spent much time talking about is using FIBCs in situations with chemicals, pharmaceuticals, and
> dangerous materials. That's because for most products and applications, a standard bulk bag will
> suffice. However, that certainly isn't always the case and at National Bulk Bag we pride ourselves in
> being an educational resource for all users and website visitors."

### Pharmaceutical applications — food grade as the entry point

> "If you're dealing with pharmaceutical grade materials the first type of bag to inquire about is food
> grade bags."

> "Food grade bags are specially manufactured to meet certain standards that make them perfect when
> product contamination is absolutely unacceptable."

> "Food grade bags are manufactured with very specific standards in mind. Two of the most important are
> BRC and SQF."

**BRC**, defined:
> "BRC certification refers to the British Retail Consortium, a leading safety and quality certification
> program that is used by over 23,000 suppliers in 120+ countries."

**SQF**, defined:
> "SQF refers to Safe Quality Food and is one of the top safety management systems recognized by
> retailers and food-service providers in the world."

The stated logic for using food-grade bags in pharma:
> "These standards mean these specific bags meet much higher manufacturing standards that make them safe
> for food applications. Because of this, these bags are often ideal for pharmaceutical companies that
> are usually required to adhere to very similar standards."

`UNVERIFIED:` The site's pharma position is that **food-grade certification is the proxy for pharma
grade**. It never claims a distinct pharmaceutical certification, GMP compliance, or FDA registration.

### UN certified FIBCs for hazardous materials

> "For those applications, there are UN Certified FIBCs. These bags are specifically designed and
> manufactured to hold and safely transport hazardous materials."

> "On top of the extensive testing procedures these bags go through, they are specifically labeled to
> easily identify the contents of the bag."

> "This is extremely important as this labeling notifies the handler of not only the contents but where
> the bag has been manufactured, the weight of the bag, and other critical information."

**Not stated:** the UN specification code itself (e.g. 13H3/13H4), which regulatory body issues it,
drop-test or specific test protocols, or packing group designations. "Extensive testing procedures" is
the extent of the description.

### High temperature products

The section contains **no substantive body text** — only a cross-link:

> "For all things related to high temperature products and bulk bags, [check out this blog post where we
> wrote all about it.]"

`UNVERIFIED:` No temperature figures, thresholds, or heat-resistant specs are given. The linked post was
not fetched.
