# FIBC / Bulk Bag Construction and Fabric — National Bulk Bag

## Scope and reliability

**Yield from the four assigned pages.** All four were fetched. Three of them
(`/bulk-bags/`, `/fibc/`, `/custom-bulk-bags/`) are marketing landing pages and
carry almost no numeric specification content — they name option *categories*
(loop styles, top styles, bottom styles, Type A/B/C/D, baffles) without
dimensions, fabric weights, denier, GSM, SWL tiers, or safety factors. The
`/products/` page is the only assigned page with hard data, and it carries it in
the faceted filter sidebar and in individual product detail pages.

To make this file usable as sales reference material, I fetched additional pages
on the same two company domains. **Every section below cites the exact URL its
content came from.** Content from `blog.nationalbulkbag.com` is labelled as such
so it can be weighted differently from the main product site if needed.

**Quotation convention.** Text in `"double quotes"` is verbatim from the page.
Unquoted text is the fetch extractor's rendering of page content and may be
lightly reworded — treat unquoted claims as accurate in substance but do not
paste them into copy as a quotation. Anything I could not confirm is marked
`UNVERIFIED:`.

## Body construction types

Sources:
<https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/types-of-jumbo-bag-construction-upanel-circular-4panel>,
<https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/what-are-the-different-types-fibc-construction>

### Circular (tubular)

- "tubular piece of fabric that forms all sides of the bag allowing for stitching only for the top and bottom"
- Also stated as: "one 'tubular' pieces of fabric that forms all sides off the bag allowing for the stitching only at the top and bottom."
- Seams: top and bottom only.
- Naming clarification, verbatim: "They aren't literally circular; they are simply constructed with a single piece of fabric that forms all four sides"
- Shape retention: "will hold that shape the least of the construction types when filled" — lowest of the three.

### U-Panel (also written U+2)

- "three body panels – one that runs the full length of the bag forming the bottom and two sides and two additional pieces of fabric sewn into the larger piece that form the other two sides"
- Verbatim naming: "3 body panels (often referred to as U+2 to reflect the single piece of material sewn together with the 2 side panels)."
- Product detail pages use the type label "U+2 Panel Bag" (see 35x35x35 Spout/Spout coated below).
- Positioning, verbatim: "industry standard and the most popular type of construction offering high safe working loads (SWLs) and solid durability"
- SWL: "U-panel bags tend to have the best ratings" for safe working load.
- `UNVERIFIED:` one extraction rendered U-Panel as "5 total pieces". The pages
  themselves say "three body panels" / "3 body panels". Use **three panels**.

### 4-Panel

- "four individual pieces sewn together to form a square structure."
- Seams/drawback, verbatim: "large amount of stitching required to keep the bag together. This can allow for more areas of the bag prone to failure."
- Shape retention: "will hold their shape better than the U-panel bags".
- Application, verbatim: "ideal for storage due to the way they hold their shape when filled."

### 8-Panel

Not explained in prose on any page fetched, but it is a live catalog facet with
4 SKUs, all baffle bags — see the 8-Panel table below.
`UNVERIFIED:` no page describes how an 8-panel bag is constructed.

### Ranking statements (verbatim)

- Shape retention: "4-panel bags will hold their square shape the best, followed by U-panel, then circular."
- Important qualifier on SWL, verbatim: "other bag constructions can still have higher SWLs as SWL often takes into consideration GSM fabric, lift loop design, and sewing patterns."

### What counts as "standard"

Source: <https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/what-is-standard-bulk-bag>

- Verbatim: "When it comes to the bulk bag industry, beyond FIBCA, there is no central bag authority that defines standards."
- Most common sizes named: "The 35x35x35, 35x35x40, 35x35x50, and sometimes the 37x37x63".
- Most common configuration: "duffle top (for easy filling) and spout bottom (for controlled discharge)"; also common is "spout top, spout bottom construction".
- Most common body construction: "Circular and U-panel construction"; for baffled bags, "4-panel is the most common".

## UV stabilization

Sources: <https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-construction/what-is-uv-stabilization-how-does-it-impact-fibc-bulk-bags>,
<https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/>

- Glossary, verbatim: "An additive is added to the resin prior to extrusion of the yarns to provide this protection."
- Verbatim: "Polymer stabilizers are a class of chemical additives commonly added to polymers during the manufacturing process to inhibit degradation of the material."
- Mechanism, verbatim: stabilizers "give the bags resistance to UV rays by absorbing the rays and dissipating them as low-level heat."
- Point of addition, verbatim: "When FIBCs are made, the polypropylene is mixed with polymer stabilizers."
- **Exposure limit, verbatim: "Our bags at NBB adhere to the ISO 21898 Standard which states that bag exposure to UV rays should never exceed 200 hours."**
- Caveat, verbatim: "It should be noted that these stabilizers do break down over time and you should consult your supplier/distributor about your bag's rating (usually presented in hours of exposure)."
- Degradation mechanism, verbatim: "UV rays interact with the chemical bonds that hold polypropylene together creating free radicals that weaken those bonds. This process in turn causes the material to break down, crack and become unstable."

### Loop over loop (edge corner / corner seam loops)

- Verbatim: "each corner has a lift loop sewn into the seam of the bag"

- Most cost-effective option.
- May be harder for forklift operators to use.

### Cross corner

- Verbatim: loops "sewn into eight points in opposite corners"
- Verbatim: requires circular fabric construction.
- Verbatim: "loops stand upright, making it easier for forklift operators to grab and lift"
- Usually more expensive than loop over loop.
- `/bulk-bags/` phrasing, verbatim: "sewn into the corners of the bag"
- `UNVERIFIED:` the blog says cross corner "requires circular fabric construction", but the catalog shows cross corner loops on U-Panel, 4-Panel and 8-Panel SKUs. Treat the blog statement as unreliable.

### Stevedore straps

- Glossary, verbatim: "Heavy-duty straps (usually 2 as a single pair) that are threaded through each set of lift loops on opposing sides of the bag to create a single lift point."
- Three subtypes, verbatim:
  - *One Stevedore Loop* — "a loop pulled through the four standard corner loops to connect all of them"
  - *Double Stevedore Loops* — "two loops that connect each parallel loop at the corners"
  - *Supporting Stevedore Loops* — "supporting loops that are attached to each corner loop"
- Enable lifting from one or two loops instead of four.
- More expensive specialty option.

- Named only on `/bulk-bags/`, verbatim: "A single cover connecting two corners"

## Safe Working Load (SWL) and Safety Factor

Sources: <https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-glossary/>,
<https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/fibc-bulk-bag-safety-51-vs-61>,
<https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/products/>,
<https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/multi-trip-bag-program/>

### Definitions (verbatim)

- SWL: "the amount of load, (in pounds or kilograms), which a bag is designed to carry. Note: Most customers use the metric system where 1 ton equals 2205 pounds or 1000 kilograms."
- SWL (blog): "the maximum load limit that can be safely carried by a bulk bag without risking damage or injury."
- Safety Factor: "Also referred to as a Service Factor, it is an industry standard requiring the FIBC to handle five or six times its Safe Working Load, (SWL), normally written as a ratio, \"5:1 or 6:1 SF\"."
- 5:1 SFR: "they have the ability to hold five times the amount of their safe work load (SWL)"
- 6:1 SFR: "they have the ability to hold six times their rated safe working load"

### Single-trip vs multi-trip

- Single Trip Bag, glossary verbatim: "An FIBC designed in accordance with ISO 21898."
- 5:1 = single use, verbatim: "Most woven polypropylene bulk bags are manufactured for one use/one trip"
- 6:1 = multi use, verbatim: "Some woven polypropylene bulk bags are specifically manufactured for multiple uses"
- **Warning, verbatim: "Never use a 5:1 safety ratio bag for more than ONE use/trip"**
- Verbatim: "using a 5:1 SFR bag is dangerous and can result in a unsafe working environment" if used for multiple trips (in reference to ISO 21898).
- For 6:1 reuse, FIBCA recommends cleaning, reconditioning, inspection for damage, tracking usage, and random testing protocols before reuse.

### Multi-trip program (EcoTrace™)

Source: <https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/multi-trip-bag-program/>

- **"6:1 rating"**, constructed with **"heavyweight fabric"**.
- Reuse life, verbatim: **"10-12 times, with some reaching up to 20 uses"**

- Environmental claim: reduces "CO2 emissions and landfill waste".
- Visual identifier, verbatim: **"light blue fabric"**.

## Static protection — Type A / B / C / D

Sources: <https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-safety/static-electricity-in-fibcs-how-to-avoid-the-dangers-of-storing-combustible-products>,
<https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/custom-bulk-bags/>

`/custom-bulk-bags/` lists "Type A B C & D bulk bags" as a custom capability but
gives no detail. All description below is from the blog.

| Type | Fabric (verbatim) | Static behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | "made of plain-woven polypropylene and other non-conductive fabrics." | No static protection. Verbatim: "do NOT offer static protection and should NOT be used to store or transport flammable or combustible products." Organic materials and chemicals can form combustible clouds ignitable by electrostatic discharge from a Type A bag. |
| **B** | "made of plain-woven polypropylene fabrics or other non-conductive material." | No dissipation mechanism, but has "low breakdown voltage to prevent incidents where highly energetic and dangerous propagating brush discharges (PBD) occur." Not considered anti-static; not suitable for flammable atmospheres. |
| **C** (conductive / groundable) | "made from non-conductive polypropylene fabrics interwoven with conducting threads." | Threads "must be connected to a designated ground during filling and emptying to safely dissipate static electricity." Safe for flammable powders **only when properly grounded**. Verbatim risk note: "Ensuring that the type C bag is grounded properly is subject to human error." |
| **D** (static dissipative) | "made from anti-static and static dissipative fabrics that safely prevent sparks and brush discharges without needing a ground connection." | Contains "quasi-conductive yarns that dissipate static electricity into the atmosphere." Crohmiq™ / CROHMIQ is named as the exemplar. Verbatim benefit: "Using type D bags eliminates the possibility of human error associated with the manufacturing and use of groundable type C bags." |

Summary, verbatim: **"only Type C and Type D bags are truly safe for storing and
transporting flammable and combustible materials."**

`UNVERIFIED:` no Type A/B/C/D SKUs appear in the `/products/` facets — the
in-stock catalog has no static-type filter. Types appear to be a custom-order
capability only.
