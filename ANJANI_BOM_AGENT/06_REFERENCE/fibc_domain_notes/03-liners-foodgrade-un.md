## 1. Liner Types — Construction Categories

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-liners/ · https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-liners/revisiting-fibc-liners · https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/a-quick-guide-to-fibc-bulk-bag-liners

The vendor groups liners into four construction categories. Note the naming inconsistency
between the product page and the blog (recorded below, not reconciled).

| Type | Vendor definition (verbatim) | Source page |
|---|---|---|
| **Lay-Flat / Gusseted / Standard** | "cylindrical in shape, open at the top, and the bottom is often heat sealed" (product page); "Also referred to as standard or lay-flat liners, gusseted liners are the most common type of FIBC liner. They are cylindrical in shape, open at the top with a heat-sealed bottom (that is cut during discharge)." (blog) | fibc-liners/ ; revisiting-fibc-liners |
| **Tubular** | Opening at the bottom rather than a sealed base. "The end of the liner is simply fed through the discharge spout and tied during manufacturing preventing the need to feed and cut the liner during discharge." | revisiting-fibc-liners |
| **Form Fit** | "specifically designed to take the exact form of the bag including the spout inlet and outlet" (product page); "designed and manufactured to take the exact form of the bag they are placed in – including the top and bottom construction (spout, duffle, etc.)." (blog) | fibc-liners/ ; revisiting-fibc-liners |
| **Baffle** | "form fitted to the FIBC and makes use of internal baffles to maintain a square shape"; "manufactured with built in baffles…form fit to the bag they are pre-inserted into and therefore cannot be sold individually." | fibc-liners/ ; revisiting-fibc-liners |
| **Aluminum Foil** | "Form shaped aluminum liners, also known as foil liners." Offer "great moisture, oxygen, and UV protection." | fibc-liners/ |

**Naming note (UNVERIFIED reconciliation):** the main product page calls the first category
"Lay-Flat Liners"; the blog calls the same thing "Gusseted Liners" and states lay-flat/standard
are alternate names for it. Treat lay-flat = gusseted = standard as the same construction per
`revisiting-fibc-liners`. The main product page lists Aluminum Foil as a fifth top-level type;
the blog treats aluminum as a **material**, not a construction.

### 1.1 Lay-Flat / gusseted liner properties

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-liners/ · https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/a-quick-guide-to-fibc-bulk-bag-liners

Barrier / performance capabilities listed for this liner type:

- "Oxygen barrier"
- "Moisture barrier"
- "Chemical resistance"
- "Anti-static properties"
- "High Strength"

`UNVERIFIED:` The site lists "Anti-static properties" as a bullet under lay-flat liners but
gives **no** antistatic specification — no surface resistivity value, no conductive/dissipative
grade, no additive named, and no statement of which FIBC type (A/B/C/D) an antistatic liner
pairs with. This is the site's only mention of antistatic liners found across all pages scraped.

### 1.2 Form fit liner properties

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/a-quick-guide-to-fibc-bulk-bag-liners · https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-liners/

- "do not have folds, pleats or other traps to slow down product flow"
- Offer consistent filling/discharge performance
- "protect the product packed from contamination during processing, storage, and transportation"

### 1.3 Baffle liner properties

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/a-quick-guide-to-fibc-bulk-bag-liners

- Use internal baffles to maintain square shape
- Can "hold up to 30% more product in a given space"
- Improve stackability and pallet efficiency
- Cannot be sold individually — supplied only pre-inserted (`revisiting-fibc-liners`)

### 1.4 Aluminum / foil liner properties

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-liners/ · https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/a-quick-guide-to-fibc-bulk-bag-liners · https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-liners/revisiting-fibc-liners

- Aluminum-laminated construction, "form shaped"
- "offer superior protection against moisture and UV degradation"
- Provide "great moisture, oxygen, and UV protection"
- Available with spout options for filling/discharge
- "manufactured using various aluminum compounds depending on the specific needs of each application"

`UNVERIFIED:` No specific foil laminate structure, foil gauge, or barrier test value (OTR/MVTR)
is published. **EVOH is never mentioned anywhere on the site** across all pages scraped — do not
claim EVOH barrier liners from this source.

### 1.5 Tubular vs gusseted — operational difference

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/bulk-bag-liners/what-is-a-tubular-fibc-bulk-bag-liner

> "Tubular liners are very similar to your standard gusset liners in many ways. They are made of
> the same polyethylene material and are pre-inserted into the bag before leaving the
> manufacturing facility."

> "In a standard gusset liner, the bottom of the liner is sealed shut. When discharging a bag,
> this liner is pulled through the bottom of the spout and cut allowing discharge to take place.
> In a tubular liner, there is no bottom seal to cut. The end of the liner is simply fed through
> the spout before the bag is tied and there is no need to cut the liner when discharging."

**Purchasing availability by liner type:**

| Liner type | How it can be purchased |
|---|---|
| Tubular | "pre-inserted into the bags before purchase" only |
| Gusseted | "can be purchased in rolls and inserted by the purchaser or purchased pre-inserted into the bags before purchase" |
| Form-fit | "only available pre-inserted into the bag before purchased" |
| Baffle | Pre-inserted only — "cannot be sold individually" (`revisiting-fibc-liners`) |

**Cost:** "Generally speaking, tubular and gusseted liners cost less than form fit liners."

**Application fit:** Tubular liners work particularly well with spout-bottom bags.

## 2. Liner Attachment Methods — Full Comparison

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/comparing-different-liner-attachment-options-for-bulk-bag-applications

Article framing: "Liner attachment directly impacts fill speed, operator involvement, dust
control, and downtime."

Five attachment methods are compared:

| # | Method | Description | Pros | Cons | Best for | Operational impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Loose drop-in** | Liner inserted without attachment | "Lowest upfront cost"; compatible with existing inventory; flexible across multiple SKUs | "High operator involvement"; liner can shift during filling; increased dust risk | Manual or low-speed operations | Heavy labor dependency; slippage requires frequent operator adjustment |
| 2 | **Tabbed (tacked)** | "Lightly sewn or glued to interior seams using small tabs" | "Better stability than loose liners"; reduced repositioning; moderate cost increase | Some movement possible under pressure; limited fill spout support | Semi-automated lines; moderate production volumes | "Practical balance between performance and cost for mid-sized manufacturers" |
| 3 | **Sewn-in** | "Stitched directly into bag construction around seams or spout" | "Strong liner stability"; improved dust containment; "consistent alignment for automated filling"; reduced product migration | Slightly higher bag cost; less flexibility for frequent spec changes | High-speed filling; food-grade applications; dust-sensitive materials | Reduced operator adjustments and housekeeping; faster payback for throughput-focused plants |
| 4 | **Form-fit / structured** | Liner matching internal bag geometry; integrates with filling equipment via flanged tops | "Improved equipment integration"; faster consistent fills; better product evacuation; reduced air entrapment | Higher upfront cost; requires equipment coordination; less SKU flexibility | Automated, high-volume environments | Addresses air pockets, densification issues, weight inconsistencies |
| 5 | **Glue-attached** | Liner adhered using adhesive bonding | More stable than loose; lower cost than sewn; reduced shifting | "Adhesive durability may vary in humid environments"; less structural support than stitched designs | Controlled indoor operations with moderate product weight | Cost-attractive but requires validation under real production conditions |

**Selection guidance (verbatim conclusion):**
> "A slightly higher liner cost can often reduce cleanup labor, downtime, product waste, and
> customer complaints."

The article directs buyers to evaluate **total cost of ownership rather than unit price alone**,
across: labor efficiency, fill speed consistency, dust control, compliance risk, and operational
integration.

**Cross-reference — attachment tabs (main product page):** liners "can be manufactured with
attachment tabs which allow the liners to be sewed, tied, or glued to the bag."
(https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-liners/)

### 3.3 Polyethylene vs polypropylene liners — temperature

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/what-are-polypropylene-liners

> "In specific situations, a standard polyethylene liner isn't sufficient. Generally speaking a
> standard polyethylene liner won't suffice in situations involving high temperature products."

> "Polypropylene liners are for situations that require a liner that can withstand temperatures
> higher than 170°F."

| Item | Maximum product temperature |
|---|---|
| Polyethylene liner | **170°F** (begins softening at **195°F**) |
| Standard FIBC, no liner | **200°F** |
| Polypropylene liner | **295°F** — "can withstand product temperatures of up to 295°F" |

Additional properties: polypropylene liners are "generally…thicker than a standard polyethylene
liner and are sturdier." Availability: "They aren't very common as they are only used in very
specific situations." Used in food, pharmaceutical, and chemical industries where contamination
prevention is critical.

## 4. When a Liner Is Needed

### 4.1 Four common uses

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/four-common-uses-for-fibc-bulk-bag-liners

1. **Fine powders** — when sift-proofing alone is insufficient, a "polyethylene liner can be a
   great solution." Liners provide dual barrier function: "keeping moisture in as well as keeping
   moisture out – which can be extremely important when working with fine powders." Can be
   manually inserted or pre-manufactured into bags.
2. **Food grade products** — "Food grade liners can help keep product fresh and free from
   contamination" for powdered, granular, or dry flowable foods. Also: "liners can help with
   discharge of product so the end user gets all contents out of the bag."
3. **Pharmaceutical products** — "Many pharmaceutical products are sensitive to elements such as
   moisture and UV rays making liners essential when transporting these materials."
4. **Over-the-bag application** — an alternative configuration where "polyethylene liners [are]
   put under the bag before it is filled." After filling, "the poly liner is pulled over the bag
   and tied," functioning as "an extra barrier when product is shipped."

### 4.2 Polyethylene liner use cases

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/when-to-use-a-polyethylene-liner

Definition:
> "Polyethylene liners, commonly referred to as poly liners, are flexible plastic liners
> specifically designed to line the inside of a flexible intermediate bulk container (FIBC or
> bulk bag)."

Four stated applications:

1. **Chemical protection** — prevents chemicals from penetrating fabric weaves and seams; reduces
   wear on the polypropylene material.
2. **Fine powder containment** — "Fine powders are more susceptible to leaking through the bag
   during transport, maneuvering and filling of the bag." Liners should be tied at the top after
   filling.
3. **Sensitive material protection** — "Poly liners prevent moisture, dust and other contaminates
   from reaching the materials in the FIBC."
4. **Regulated industries** — food and pharmaceutical sectors, due to strict contamination
   regulations.

Tool referenced (not scraped): "FIBC Bulk Bag Liner Estimation Calculator" for sizing.

### 4.3 Industries using lined FIBCs

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/a-quick-guide-to-fibc-bulk-bag-liners

Agriculture, chemical, pharmaceutical, pet food, metals & mining.
"Bulk bags with poly liners are often used for food products and very fine powders."
Main product page adds: "This powerful combination is often used for food grade products and
very fine powders." (https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-liners/)

## 5. Liner vs Lined Bag vs Coated Bag — Decision Criteria

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-liners/when-to-use-lined-fibcs-liners-coated-fibcs

### 5.1 Removable poly liner

- "Poly liners are plastic liners inserted into FIBCs and provide protection against leaking."
- Best for products requiring fine powder containment or additional moisture barriers.
- Particularly suited to food and pharmaceutical industries **because they're removable** —
  allowing bag reuse with a fresh liner.
- Key advantage: flexibility for reuse across different products/applications.

### 5.2 Lined bulk bag (pre-installed liner)

- Liner already built into the woven polypropylene fabric, "either sewn into seams or tabbed."
- Four main liner types available (see §1), enabling use with varied products.
- Primary disadvantage: "The liners are usually either sewn into the seams of the bag or tabbed
  to the bag's material and cannot be removed" — limiting reusability vs removable liners.

### 5.3 Coated FIBC

- What coating is: before assembly, "an additional polypropylene film is added to the bag's
  fabric sealing the small gaps in the poly weaves."
- Application location: typically applied **internally**, to prevent powder particles from
  getting stuck in the weave during discharge.
- Field test to detect coating: "Press the weave together to see if it spreads apart…If the weave
  does not spread apart, there's a good chance the bag is coated."
- Benefit: protects against external contaminants — dust, moisture, dirt.
- Common applications: powder-form products — cement, detergents, flour, salt, carbon black,
  sand, sugar. Sometimes combined with poly liners for extra protection.

### 5.4 Decision rules (verbatim)

> "Coated bags don't offer as much protection against outside contamination as lined bags and
> poly liners. However, they are a very economical option."

> "Fine powders can seep through the woven strands making standard and coated bags ineffective
> with these products."

**Removable liner vs pre-installed liner:**
- Choose **removable** liners if reusing bags across different products.
- Choose **pre-installed** liners if specialty options are needed — aluminum foil, lay-flat,
  baffle, form fit.

### 5.5 Sift-resistant seams — the alternative to a liner

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bag-construction/specialty-bulk-bags-fibc-big-bags-with-sift-resistant-seams

Why sifting occurs:
> "During the manufacturing process, sewing machine needles make small holes in the fabric of the
> bag." and "very fine materials/products may seep through the weave or the sew lines causing a
> loss of product and creating quite a mess."

Construction:
> "Inserting a sift-proof/sift-resistant seam into the bag involves using a felt or spongy type
> yarn in the sewing process. The sewing operator feeds the sift cord along the seam of the bag
> when sewing, filling the areas near the needle hole in the seam."

Most robust design: "one strip of felt between the polypropylene seam with filler cord on the top
and bottom of the seam."

When used: for "very fine powders such as milk powders, iron powders and/or resins."

Economics: "In most cases, coated bags are the most cost-effective option and work for most
products."

**Critical caveat (verbatim):** "No bag is 100% sift-proof."

`UNVERIFIED:` No micron or mesh particle-size threshold is published anywhere on the site to
define when a product counts as a "fine powder" requiring a liner. The distinction is qualitative
only.

## 6. Food Grade Bulk Bags — Materials and FDA Status

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-bulk-bags/ · https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/food-grade-bulk-bags-3-myths-and-facts

### 6.1 Virgin resin requirement

- "100% virgin polypropylene resin" is used in manufacturing, which "meets FDA food grade
  requirements." (main food grade page)
- Glossary definition of virgin resin: "100% virgin resin" = "polypropylene resin that has never
  been used." (https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/fibc-bulk-bags/a-guide-to-flexible-intermediate-bulk-container-vocabulary)
- "Food grade bulk bags must be manufactured with 100% virgin polypropylene resin."
  (top-5-things article)

### 6.2 The FDA position — most important compliance fact on the site

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/food-grade-bulk-bags-3-myths-and-facts

**Myth #1 — "FDA approved bag":**
> "Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved bags do not exist. The FDA does not issue any
> approval, certificate, or accreditation for FIBCs or FIBC liners."

What the FDA actually does:
> The FDA recognizes that "100% virgin polypropylene resin 'may be safely used as articles or
> components of articles intended for use in contact with food.'"
> Reference cited on the page: **21 CFR 177.1520**

**Myth #2 — GFSI accreditation:**
> "GFSI **DOES NOT** make policy for retailers, manufacturers OR undertake any accreditation or
> certification activities."

GFSI instead recognizes third-party certifications: BRC Global Standard for Packaging &
Packaging Materials, SQF Code (Safe Quality Food Institute), FSSC 22000, IFS PAC secure.

**Myth #3 — food grade liner does not make a bag food grade:**
> "Not all FIBCs are created equal and just because you are using a food grade liner with a bag
> does not mean any bag can be used for food grade products."

Bags contacting food must be "manufactured with 100% virgin polypropylene resin" to prevent
contamination from recycled materials.

**Supplier red flag (verbatim, top-5-things article):** "Avoid suppliers making false claims such
as FDA approved bulk bags."

### 6.3 Clean room manufacturing controls

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-bulk-bags/ · https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/food-grade-fibcs-brc-certification · https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/what-are-sqf-bulk-bags-and-when-do-you-need-them

The same control list is repeated near-verbatim across the food grade page, the BRC article, and
the SQF article — treat it as the vendor's canonical clean-room checklist:

| # | Control |
|---|---|
| 1 | Proper ventilation |
| 2 | Pest control procedures |
| 3 | **Metal detection** |
| 4 | **Light table inspection** (visual inspection) |
| 5 | Proper personnel wardrobe |
| 6 | Proper personnel hygiene |
| 7 | **HACCP program** (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) |
| 8 | Record keeping |

The `top-5-things` article adds one further item to this list: **vacuum air cleaning**.

Food grade bags are "Produced in certified cleanroom environments that are regularly inspected."
(https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/food-grade-vs-feed-grade)

### 6.4 Facility certifications held

Source: https://www.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-bulk-bags/

Food grade bags are manufactured in facilities holding:
- **BRCGS** (British Retail Consortium Global Standards)
- **FSSC 22000**
- **SQF** (Safe Quality Food Institute)
- **IFS** (International Featured Standards)

These meet "strict requirements set forth by both GFSI (Global Food Safety Initiative)" for food,
chemical, and pharmaceutical sectors.

### 6.5 Advantages claimed for food grade bulk bags

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/food-grade-bulk-bags-a-great-choice-for-commercial-food-companies

1. **FDA-compliant materials** — "Food grade bulk bags are made from 100% virgin polypropylene
   resin, which meets standards outlined by the FDA."
2. **BRC certification** — "BRC certification became a GFSI (Global Food Safety Initiative)
   recognized standard in 1998." GFSI also recognizes SQFI, FSSC 22000, and IFS.
3. **Cost effectiveness** — bulk bags hold up to 4,000 pounds; "can be safely handled by numerous

## 7. Food Grade vs Feed Grade, and Food-Safe Resins

### 7.1 Food grade vs feed grade

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/food-grade-vs-feed-grade

> "One of the most common mix-ups? The terms 'feed grade' and 'food grade.' They sound similar,
> but they're not the same"

| Dimension | Feed grade | Food grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Intended consumer** | Animals — pets, livestock, zoo animals | Humans — "sugar, flour, rice, spices, and nutritional supplements" |
| **Resin** | "May include recycled resins or uncertified components"; not approved for human food contact | "Made from virgin resin, which means the plastic hasn't been recycled and is free from unknown contaminants" |
| **Materials used** | — | Polypropylene, polyethylene liners, approved coatings |
| **Facility** | No cleanroom requirement; standard manufacturing protocols | "Produced in certified cleanroom environments that are regularly inspected" |
| **Certifications** | None required | FDA, BRC, AIB, or SQF programs |
| **Cost** | More cost-effective | Higher |

**Contamination tolerance:** food-grade bags may "contain trace amounts of things like mold (up to
100 parts per million)," which are considered safe for human consumption.
`UNVERIFIED:` The 100 ppm mold figure is stated without a citation to any regulation or standard.

**Selection criteria:** product destination (animal vs human), direct food contact, temperature
exposure, and regulatory requirements for the packaged product.

### 7.2 Food grade plastic resins (general guide, not FIBC-specific)

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/safe-food-grade-plastic/

> "Food grade plastic is a material considered safe for contact with food and beverages. It meets
> strict regulatory standards for additives, dyes, and chemical stability."

Requirements: must not contain harmful chemicals like BPA; must remain durable without breakdown
during normal storage; must prevent chemical leaching into food.

| Resin | Code | Properties | Applications | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PET** (Polyethylene Terephthalate) | 1 | Lightweight, strong | Soda bottles, single-serve containers | "Not recommended for reuse due to potential toxin leaching" |
| **HDPE** (High Density Polyethylene) | 2 | Durable, chemical-resistant | Milk jugs, bulk food containers | Approved for high-contact food storage |
| **LDPE** (Low Density Polyethylene) | 4 | Flexible, moisture-resistant | Bags and wraps | — |
| **PP** (Polypropylene) | 5 | Heat-resistant, microwave-safe | Storage boxes, yogurt containers | — |

Guidance: "always look for plastics marked with recycling numbers 2, 4, or 5" as safest for food
storage. Explicitly warns **against PVC and polystyrene** — they release harmful chemicals or
degrade over time. Cites FDA regulations for food contact materials; recommends tracking certified
materials and conducting regular audits.

`UNVERIFIED:` This article covers food storage plastics generally. It does not state which of
these resins apply to FIBC liners specifically. Do not map recycling codes onto liner products.

### 7.3 ProvisionGard™ / PVGard insect-resistant additive

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/what-is-provisiongard-pvgard

## 8. GFSI and the Recognized Certification Schemes

### 8.1 What GFSI is and is not

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/food-grade-fibc-bulk-bags/gfsi-standards-for-fibc-bulk-bags

> "GFSI, or Global Food Safety Initiative, is an initiative that brings together key actors of the
> food industry to collaboratively drive continuous improvement in food management safety systems
> around the world."

- Established **May 2000** by major food retailers through the Food Business Forum, to address
  inconsistent food safety standards after 1990s crises like BSE and listeria.
- **Critical limitation (verbatim):** "GFSI is not a regulatory body. They do not set required
  standards or undertake any accreditation or certification programs."
- GFSI instead recognizes certain food standards and establishes benchmarking requirements for
  official recognition.
- Scope: food safety only — excludes animal safety and environmental standards.
- **Verbatim:** "There is no such thing as a 'GFSI certified' bag distributor and/or manufacturer.
  However, the BRC, SQFI, FSSC and IFS all certify facilities."

GFSI's four stated objectives (from the rules-and-regulations article): reducing food safety
risks, managing costs, developing competencies, creating stakeholder collaboration.

### 9.5 The final four tests — drop, topple, righting, tear

Source: https://blog.nationalbulkbag.com/un-certified-fibc-bulk-bags/un-certified-bulk-bags-rigorous-testing-procedures-part-2-of-2

Article scope statement: "This second part covers the last four tests required for UN
certification: the drop test, topple test, righting test, and tear test."

#### Drop test — heights by packaging group

| Packaging group | Drop height |
|---|---|
| **PG I** | **70.9 inches** |
| **PG II** | **47.3 inches** |
| **PG III** | **31.5 inches** |

- Bag is dropped on its **weakest bottom point**.
- Pass criterion: "the bag must not lose any contents. A small, quick release of material is
  allowed."

#### Topple test

- **Uses identical heights to the drop test** (PG I 70.9" / PG II 47.3" / PG III 31.5").
- Bag lands on any top portion of a smooth surface.
- Pass criterion: "the bag must keep all its contents inside, with only a brief release allowed."

#### Righting test

- Applies to bags with top or side lifting capability.
- "bag is on its side and lifted to an upright position" (category page); bag starts horizontally
  and is "lifted upright at a set speed."
- **Dual-strap bags:** one approved device lifts. **Four-strap bags:** two devices required.
- Pass criterion: "the bag and its lifting straps must not be damaged in a way that makes them
  unsafe to use."

#### Tear test

- Cut: a knife creates "a 45-degree cut halfway between the bottom and the top level of the
  contents." (Category page wording: "bag wall is punctured with a knife at a 45-degree angle
  halfway between the bottom of the bag and top level of the contents.")
- **Part one:** "A heavy load (**twice the safe working limit**) is placed on the bag. It must stay
  together for at least **five minutes**."
- **Part two:** "The bag is lifted and held in the air for **five minutes**. It must not fall
  apart."
- Pass criterion: "the cut must not grow more than **25% longer** than it was at the start."

`UNVERIFIED:` The cut length itself is not specified — only the maximum permitted growth (25%).
