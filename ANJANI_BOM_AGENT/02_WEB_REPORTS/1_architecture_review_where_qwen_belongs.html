Anjani Interweave Pvt Ltd FIBC BOM Generation System Architecture review 9 Aug 2026

Where Qwen belongs

Your architecture is sound and should be kept. But it is pointed at the wrong target, and that is why Qwen looks underused. The BOM is not a forward calculation from size and load — it is an inverse problem solved against a target weight. Once you model it that way, the model's proper job turns out to be far larger than predicting bag weight, and still stops short of generating the sheet.

Your current fine-tuning data (bom_finetuning_dataset_corrected.jsonl, 559 rows) has an output field that covers block 2 only. No costing, no loom requirement, no procurement, no specification echo. Your instinct that Qwen is being underused is correct and it is measurable: it is being trained on roughly one sixth of the deliverable.

But look at the right-hand column. Five of the six blocks are arithmetic and regrouping. Only block 1 — the specification — is decision-making. And block 1 is the one nothing in your system currently generates.

What the engineer actually decided on this sheet

Your example query gives six facts. The sheet contains around fifty. Everything below was supplied by the engineer, not the customer:

  • Bag type: U-Panel (Corner Loop). The query says only "open top flat bottom". Construction was inferred — and per the variance analysis, this single choice drives every downstream figure.
  • Loop geometry: 50 mm belt, 35 GPM, free height 25, long 65, short 35, quantity 4. The GSM chart offers "30–35 GRAM" for this load band; the engineer took the top of the band.
  • Raw-material blend: PP 88.5% + CC 10% + UV 1.5%. Nobody asked for UV.
  • Yarn: 3000 DNR, Heracle+Safety, blue & white. Static protection: Type A. Packing: 500 pcs/pallet, 48 pallets.
  • Everything switched off: no baffle, no liner, no printing, no doc pocket, no stevedore, no filler cord, no B-lock, uncoated. Twelve "NO" decisions, each of which had to be made.
The reframe

The intellectual work in a BOM is filling in the forty-four things the customer did not say. The arithmetic that follows is worth almost nothing — it is six formulas applied consistently. Your architecture treats the arithmetic as the product and the decisions as an input-parsing detail. That is inverted.

The lesson, in the document's own words

"Two invented components (185 g) offset a 259 g missing component and a 66 g belt shortfall. A plausible total concealed a structurally wrong BOM. Totals must never be used to validate a BOM; every line must be checked independently."

Note what the root cause was: not arithmetic, not a missing formula. The agent misclassified the construction at step one and never re-tested that assumption. Its own memory file already held three of the four correct formulas. This was a retrieval failure caused by a classification failure.

That tells you exactly where model capability is worth spending: on the decisions at the top of the sheet, and on making those decisions auditable and challengeable. Not on the multiplications at the bottom.

04 The measurement that changes the design

Everything above is reasoning about the shape of the problem. This section is a number, measured on your 559-record corpus this afternoon, and it contradicts an assumption baked into all four codebases.

Every version of the engine derives body GSM from (family, SWL, safety factor) via a load chart. So I asked the corpus: is that actually how the GSM was chosen?

For u_panel · SWL 1000 · SF 5:1 there are 102 bags in the corpus. The body GSM they were built at:

120 · 125 · 130 · 135 · 140 · 145 · 150 · 160 · 170 · 180 · 185 · 190 · 195 · 210

Fourteen distinct values across a 75% range, for one cell of the chart. Across all cells with five or more samples, the median within-cell spread is 45.8%. The load chart is not the decision rule. It is a floor that the decision has to clear.

So what is the decision rule?

The target weight. I tested three predictors of body GSM on the 283 U-panel bags, all leave-one-out, snapping the prediction to the 40 GSM values the plant actually weaves:

Predicting body GSM · u_panel · n≈279 · leave-one-out
PredictorMedian errExactWithin 5%Within 10%

The third row uses no historical lookup and no chart. It inverts your own weight formula against the customer's stated target weight, using the rule engine's own geometry — which is itself accurate to a median 0.39% against what was really cut (94.7% within 5%).

Three supporting facts from the same corpus:

  • A usable target weight is present in 92.7% of orders. SWL is missing in 8.6%. The target weight is both the more available input and the more predictive one.
  • The finished BOM lands within 1% of the stated target in 51% of orders, within 2% in 68%, within 5% in 85%. That is not coincidence. That is somebody solving for it.
  • Your variance analysis reached the same conclusion by hand: "Anjani engineers backward from a target weight. I treated bag weight as an OUTPUT. Anjani treats it as a CONSTRAINT."
Consequence for the architecture

The BOM is an inverse problem: find the build configuration whose computed weight lands on the target, subject to the load chart as a hard floor. Your current architecture — normalise, apply rules forward, validate — has no place to express that. It computes a weight and reports it. It never solves for one.

This single change is worth more than any fine-tune, and it needs no GPU.

05 Verdict on the architecture

Keep the shape. It is right, and it is more disciplined than most first attempts. Five things need to change.

KeepDual path

Deterministic left path, retrieval right path, both feeding a constrained synthesis step. This is correct and you should not redesign it. The left path working with no corpus and no model is a real property worth protecting.

KeepValidation gate

Non-negotiable, and your instinct to recompute every weight from its own geometry rather than trusting a stated number is exactly right. Add one check it is missing: line-level comparison against retrieved neighbours, because §3 proves a correct total can hide a wholly wrong BOM.

KeepAudit trail

Rule IDs, retrieved IDs, model changes, assumptions. Keep it, and extend it to cover the costing block — a wrong quote is the expensive failure, not a wrong gram.

ChangeAdd a solver loop

The pipeline is single-pass. It needs to iterate: propose a configuration → compute → compare to target weight → adjust. Two or three passes, deterministic evaluation, model-proposed adjustments. This is §4's finding turned into control flow.

ChangeScope of the output

The pipeline ends at a component table. The deliverable is six blocks. Costing, loom requirement and procurement are all deterministic once the component table exists — a renderer, not an engine. Cheap to add, and it is what makes the system usable by the plant rather than by an engineer.

ReplaceQwen's job

Currently: predict bag weight, or arbitrate a GSM. That is one number out of ninety, and it is the number that is best derived arithmetically anyway. §6 replaces it.

ReplaceXGBoost / RF path

In model/BOM the ML path exists to predict GSM, and the LLM exists to arbitrate between it, physics and history. Once GSM is derived by inverting the target weight, all three collapse into one deterministic step and a much smaller model decision. Delete the arbitration layer rather than maintaining it.

06 The job Qwen should have

One fine-tuned adapter, one output object. Not the BOM — the BuildSpec: every decision an engineer makes, before any arithmetic happens.

What the BuildSpec contains

Roughly seventy fields, all categorical, boolean, or drawn from a closed catalogue of values. Not one of them is a computed quantity.

Read from the enquiry

  • ext_L / ext_W / ext_H cm
  • swl_kg · sf · not_specified allowed
  • target_weight_g
  • customer · order_qty
  • stated_gsm if given

Classification

  • bag_family — 12 values
  • loop_type corner / cross / single / full
  • top_type · bottom_type
  • fs_closure · ds_closure
  • static_type A / B / C / D

Material selection

  • body_gsm from the 40-value catalogue
  • bottom_gsm · panel_gsm
  • laminated · lami_gsm
  • rm_blend PP / CC / UV / RP %
  • yarn_denier · yarn_type
  • fabric_colour · loop_colour

Loop geometry ← hardest

  • loop_qty · belt_width_mm
  • belt_gpm within the chart band
  • free_height_cm
  • attach_long_cm · attach_short_cm

Component switches

  • baffle · liner + micron · doc pocket
  • filler cord · B-lock · DDP · tabbing
  • stevedore · belly band · top band
  • printing sides + colours
  • spout dims where a spout exists

Commercial

  • construction_rate_key → rate card row
  • attachment_rate_keys [ ]
  • packing pcs/pallet · pallets
  • profit_pct

The hard line

Qwen may emit a choice from a catalogue. It may never emit a computed quantity. Concretely:

  • It may say body_gsm: 150. It may not say width_cm: 93, cut_cm: 263, or weight_g: 366.885.
  • It may say belt_gpm: 35, free_height_cm: 25. It may not say the loop weighs 210 g.
  • It may say rm_blend: {PP: 88.5, CC: 10, UV: 1.5}. It may not say ₹108.125/kg.
  • It may say construction_rate_key: "U-PANEL BAG OT/FB WITH FOLDING". It may not say 11.

Served under grammar-constrained decoding against the BuildSpec JSON schema, a structurally invalid or out-of-catalogue answer becomes impossible to emit rather than merely unlikely. The model's only remaining job is picking correct values — and every value it picks is then checked by the gate against the load chart, the loom table and the retrieved neighbours.

Why this is the answer to your question

This gives Qwen roughly 90% of the judgement in a BOM and 0% of the arithmetic. It is a far bigger job than predicting bag weight — it is the whole of what the engineer knows and none of what a calculator does. That is what "using the full power of the model" should mean here.

07 Why not let Qwen generate the sheet directly

You asked directly. The answer is no, and it is not a matter of taste — it is arithmetic about arithmetic.

A finished sheet carries about 45 numeric cells in the component table alone, and close to 90 once costing, loom requirement and procurement are included. 58.3% of the component weights in your corpus are not whole numbers — they are values like 366.885 and 259.47, produced by three-factor multiplication and division by 10,000.

A BOM is only correct if every number is correct. One wrong gram becomes a wrong kilogram price becomes a wrong quotation. So the document-level accuracy is the per-number accuracy raised to the power of the number count:

Probability the whole sheet is correct, given per-number accuracy
Per-number accuracy45 numbers (component table)90 numbers (full sheet)
99%63.8%40.5%
99.5%79.7%63.7%
99.9%95.6%91.4%
99.99%99.6%99.1%
Deterministic renderer100%100%

To ship a 4B model as the generator you would need it to do multi-digit decimal arithmetic at better than 99.9% per operation. No 4B model does that, and no amount of your data will make one do it — the arithmetic is not the part that varies between your bags.

Three further reasons, each independently sufficient:

  • Silent failure. §3 already happened to you. A generated sheet that totals plausibly is not evidence of a correct sheet, and a language model's errors are specifically the plausible kind.
  • No audit trail. When the plant rejects a BOM you need to know whether the rule, the retrieval or the judgement was wrong. A single generated blob cannot tell you.
  • Rule drift. Your rate card changes; PP price changes weekly. A model with costing baked into its weights needs retraining for a price change. A renderer needs a JSON edit.

There is one legitimate use of a full-BOM generator, and it is worth keeping: as a fallback for the families the rule engine does not model — omega, tunnel, box, container, platen — where a rough BOM flagged as unvalidated beats refusing to answer. Train it, label its output clearly, and never let it reach a customer quote without an engineer's sign-off.

The query is self-contradictory

720 g and 150 GSM cannot both be true for this bag. 150 GSM gives 878 g. 720 g would need about 112 GSM — which is below the safety floor for a corner-loop bag at SWL 1000 / SF 5:1 (140 GSM, from your own GSM- GPM VS SWL-SF.xlsx, table A4).

The plant resolved this the way experienced people do: it built at 150 GSM and wrote "Bag Weight Required = 0.88 kg" on the order. The customer's 720 g was silently renegotiated.

This is not an edge case to handle later. It is the most important thing your system has to do, and no version of the current architecture can do it, because none of them has a concept of a weight target to be infeasible against.

What the system should output for this query:

  • The complete BOM at 150 GSM, total 878.36 g, costed at ₹145.20/bag.
  • An explicit blocking notice: "Target 720 g is not achievable at SWL 1000 kg / SF 5:1. Minimum safe build is 836.6 g at 140 GSM. Customer's stated 150 GSM gives 878.4 g. Confirm which constraint to relax."
  • The alternative at 140 GSM shown alongside, since it is the closest legal answer to what was asked.

An engineer reading that has everything needed to phone the customer. That is a genuinely useful product. A single generated sheet reading "720 g" would be a fabrication, and a single sheet reading "878 g" with no explanation would look like the system ignored the request.

One thing to confirm: the 720 g figure is in your message but nowhere in 90X90X90 CM.xlsx, which specifies 0.88 kg throughout. If 720 g came from a different order, tell me — it changes nothing structural, but I would rather not build a worked example on a transcription slip. (Separately: that file's contents are 85×85×85 while its name says 90×90×90. Filename-to-content mismatches like that are worth catching in extraction.)

09 The training data this needs

You said not to worry about the data — you will build what is needed. Here is what is needed, and most of it you already have.

The BuildSpec target is derivable from the corpus with no labelling effort, because every historical sheet already records both the messy input notes and the decisions that were made from them. Field coverage in the 559-record corpus:

BuildSpec field coverage in the existing corpus
Field groupCoverageNote
Dimensions, family, colour, all component switches100%Directly usable
Loop quantity, belt width, loop colour94–96%Directly usable
SWL, safety factor91%The 9% gap is real — keep it as not_specified
Loop LL / SL44–52%The weakest field, and the one the rule engine gets least right (31% within 2 cm)
Spout dimensions, closure26–47%Only present where a spout exists — coverage is conditional, not missing
RM blend, yarn spec, static type, packing, rates0%Not extracted at all. Present in the source sheets, in the costing block your extractor skips

So the data work is three things, in order:

  1. Extend the extractor to the costing block. Columns L–O and Q–Z of every sheet hold RM blend, conversion rates, yarn spec, packing and the rate-card key. This is the single largest gap and it is pure extraction work, no labelling.
  2. Emit the BuildSpec as the training target instead of the CSV component table. Same sheets, different projection. Keep the component table as a second target for the fallback generator described in §7.
  3. Fix the split. The existing plan already found 49% verbatim leakage between train and validation, caused by duplicate workbooks on the shared drive. Group by a hash of the normalised spec, split the groups, stratify by family. Any accuracy figure computed before this fix is inflated.

Two properties of the current data are correct and must survive the rewrite: not_specified is never replaced by a guess, and target weight is never conflated with BOM total. Both were hard-won. Keep them.

On volume: 559 records, or ~696 after merging BOM/BOM/Data, is enough for a closed-schema classification task under constrained decoding. Augment toward 2,000 with machine-verified paraphrases of the enquiry text — never of the BuildSpec — and hold out real sheets only for evaluation.

10 Build order

Steps 1–4 need no GPU and deliver most of the measured gain. Do not start the fine-tune first.

  1. Make the existing system runnable2 days

    Either restore the missing bom_system/app modules or delete the README's claims. Right now the project's stated accuracy cannot be checked, and every plan built on it is built on sand.

  2. Invert the weight formula1 day · biggest single win

    Derive body GSM from (target_weight − non_panel_mass) ÷ panel_area, snapped to the 40-value catalogue, floored by the load chart. §4 measures this at median 0.00% error against 3.33% for the chart. Measure it on your own evaluation set before anything else changes.

  3. Add the solver loop and the feasibility check2 days

    Compute → compare to target → adjust → recompute, capped at three passes. When no legal configuration reaches the target, say so explicitly with the nearest legal alternative, as in §8.

  4. Build the renderer for all six blocks4 days

    Costing, loom requirement and procurement are deterministic regroupings of the component table plus the rate card. This is what turns an engineer's tool into the plant's tool, and it needs no model.

  5. Extend extraction to the costing block3 days

    RM blend, yarn spec, static type, packing, rate keys. Without this the BuildSpec has no supervision for a third of its fields.

  6. Define the BuildSpec schema and re-project the corpus2 days

    Pydantic model with extra="forbid", closed enums everywhere, then generate the JSON Schema for constrained decoding from it. Re-split by spec fingerprint.

  7. Run the baselines before training anything2 days

    Base Qwen3-4B few-shot under the same grammar constraint; the rule engine alone; rule engine plus retrieval. Skipping this is the mistake people regret — sometimes the untrained base model under a grammar is already close enough.

  8. Fine-tune the BuildSpec adapter2 days

    Qwen3-4B-Instruct, LoRA in bf16, r=16, 2 epochs, completion-only loss. Serve under vLLM with XGrammar against the BuildSpec schema. Ship only if it beats all four baselines on held-out real sheets, with hallucination rate — fields asserted that the source does not contain — as the primary metric.

  9. Optional: fallback generator for unmodelled families2 days

    Second adapter, full BOM table, for omega / tunnel / box / container / platen. Output permanently labelled unvalidated. This is the only place §7's answer becomes yes.

— Answering your three questions plainly

Is the architecture correct? Yes, in shape. The dual path, the validation gate and the audit trail are right and should be preserved. It is missing a solver loop, it stops at one sixth of the deliverable, and the code for it is not on disk.

What role should Qwen have? Generate the BuildSpec — every engineering and commercial decision, roughly seventy closed-vocabulary fields — from the raw enquiry, the retrieved neighbours and the rule floors. Then re-propose adjustments when the renderer's weight misses the target. That is most of the thinking in a BOM.

Can Qwen generate the final BOM directly? No, not as the production path. Ninety numbers must all be right and 58% of them are non-integers; a deterministic renderer gets them right by construction and a 4B model cannot. Keep a full-BOM adapter only as a labelled fallback for the families the rule engine does not cover.

The thing worth internalising is the one your own variance analysis found and then almost let go of: a plausible total is not evidence of a correct BOM. Every architectural choice above follows from taking that seriously.
