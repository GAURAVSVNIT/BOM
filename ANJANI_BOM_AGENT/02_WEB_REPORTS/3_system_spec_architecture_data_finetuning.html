Anjani Interweave Pvt Ltd FIBC BOM Generation System System specification 10 Aug 2026

Architecture, data preparation
and fine-tuning

The complete build, assuming the engineer's answers are in hand. Nine stages, three data contracts, two LoRA adapters. The central design decision: the model decides, the renderer calculates, the solver closes the loop on target weight. And the answer to your data question — every BOM sheet already contains its own enquiry, in the header block.

01 The system in one picture

02 Stage by stage

0code

Ingest and normalise

Accept the enquiry in whatever form it arrives. Text passes through. PDFs with a text layer are extracted directly. Scans and photos go through OCR. Nothing is interpreted yet — this stage only produces clean UTF-8 text plus a record of where it came from.

in WhatsApp text · email body · PDF · JPG scan  ·  out RawEnquiry{text, source_type, source_file, ocr_confidence}
1QWEN A

Extraction → OrderSpec

Read what the customer actually said. Nothing more. Every field the enquiry does not contain is emitted as null with state not_specified — never guessed, never defaulted. Unit conversion happens here (inches → cm, "gr/m2" → GSM, "1.5 KG" → 1500 g) because that is reading, not deciding. Under grammar-constrained decoding the output cannot be malformed or out-of-enum.

in RawEnquiry  ·  out OrderSpec — ~32 fields, each with {value, state, evidence_span}
2code

Retrieval — the three nearest bags we have actually built

Hard filter on family, then score. Geometric distance on (L, W, H, SWL, target weight) at 0.55, categorical match on top/bottom/loop/accessory flags at 0.30, customer identity at 0.15. Return the full BuildSpec of each neighbour, not just its weight — the model needs to see what was decided, not what it weighed.

in OrderSpec  ·  out RetrievedBOM[3] with similarity scores and source sheet IDs
3code

Rule floors — what the physics and the charts permit

Compute the constraint envelope before the model chooses inside it: minimum body GSM from the load chart, the belt width/GPM band, the mandatory component set for the family, and the list of available loom widths. These are inputs to the model, not post-hoc checks — the model should see the floor while deciding, and the gate re-checks it afterwards anyway.

in OrderSpec  ·  out RuleEnvelope{gsm_min, belt_band, mandatory[], loom_widths[], sf_implied}
4QWEN B

Configuration → BuildSpec

This is the engineer's brain. Take what the customer said, the three nearest bags, and the rule floors, and produce every decision needed to build the bag — around seventy fields, all categorical, boolean, or drawn from a closed catalogue. Construction family, loop type, body GSM, belt gramage, loop free height and attachment lengths, every optional component on or off, stitch and fold specification, packing.

Not one field is a computed quantity. The model may say body_gsm: 150; it may not say width_cm: 93.

in OrderSpec + RetrievedBOM[3] + RuleEnvelope (+ WeightFeedback on passes 2–3)  ·  out BuildSpec
5code

Renderer → PhysicalBOM

Pure arithmetic. Apply the family's geometry rules to get each component's width and cut length, apply the family's quantity rules, apply the three weight formulas, sum. Then produce the loom-requirement and procurement groupings, which are regroupings of the same table. Every number in the finished sheet is born here and nowhere else.

in BuildSpec  ·  out PhysicalBOM{components[], grand_total_g, loom_requirement[], procurement[]}
6code

Weight check and the solver loop

Compare the grand total against the customer's target. Inside tolerance — proceed. Outside — construct a WeightFeedback object naming the gap in grams and percent, the knobs the model is allowed to move and their legal ranges, and what it tried last time. Send it back to stage 4. Cap at three passes; if it has not converged by then, that is information, not a bug, and it goes into the report.

in PhysicalBOM + OrderSpec.target_weight  ·  out pass, or WeightFeedback{gap_g, gap_pct, allowed_knobs[], history[]}
7code

Feasibility gate

Before declaring failure, prove it. Compute the lightest legal build (every knob at its minimum legal value, GSM at the chart floor) and the heaviest reasonable one. If the target lies outside that interval, no configuration can reach it and the system says so explicitly, with the nearest legal alternative and the specific constraint that blocks it.

out FeasibilityReport{achievable_min_g, achievable_max_g, blocking_constraint, nearest_legal_build}
8code

Validation gate

Nothing reaches a file unvalidated. Recompute every component weight from its own geometry and overwrite if it disagrees. Check body GSM is at or above the chart floor. Check every fabric width exists in the loom table. Check the mandatory component set for the family is present and no phantom component was added. Then diff every line against the nearest retrieved neighbour and flag the differences — because a correct total can hide a wholly wrong BOM, and that has already happened once on this project.

out ValidationReport{decision: pass | pass_with_warnings | reject, checks[], repairs[]}
9code

Output writer

Emit the four-block sheet in the plant's own format — specification header, component table, production instructions, tape/loom/needle-loom specs — plus an audit JSON recording, for every field, whether it came from the customer, a rule table, a retrieved neighbour, or the model.

out bom.xlsx · bom.csv · audit.json
The invariant that makes it safe

Draw a line through the middle of the pipeline. Above it, the model makes choices from finite sets. Below it, code does arithmetic. No value ever crosses that line in the wrong direction. Everything else in this document is detail.

Why evidence matters

Every extracted value points at the substring it came from. This turns hallucination from an opinion into a check: if the span does not exist in the source, the field is wrong, automatically, with no human in the loop. It is the primary training signal for adapter A and the primary ship gate.

04 Why two adapters, not one

Both adapters are LoRA on one shared Qwen3-4B-Instruct base, loaded side by side. One set of base weights in VRAM, two small adapters swapped per call. The reason to split them is not cost — it is that they are trained to do opposite things.

The two jobs are contradictory and must not share weights
Adapter A — extractionAdapter B — configuration
JobRead what was saidDecide what was not said
Correct behaviour on a missing fieldemit not_specifiedfill it in from experience
Worst failureasserting a value the source does not containleaving a field null and stalling the renderer
Primary metrichallucination rate — must be <2%per-field accuracy vs the engineer's actual choice
Checkable against source?yes — the evidence span either exists or does notno — only against what was historically built
Training rows available395 synthesised + ~30 real (see §5)395 real, zero labelling effort
Retrain triggernew customer vocabulary, new document formatrule change, new family, engineer's practice shifts

Train one model on both and you teach it that filling in blanks is sometimes right and sometimes forbidden, with nothing in the input to distinguish the cases. The hallucination metric then becomes unmeasurable, which is the one number that decides whether the system is safe to put in front of a customer.

They also retrain on completely different schedules. Adapter A goes stale when a new customer starts sending a new document format. Adapter B goes stale when the engineer changes practice. Those events are unrelated, and coupling them means every rule change forces a re-run of the extraction eval.

05 Data preparation

You said not every BOM has a matching customer query. That is true of the raw files — and it does not matter, because of where the enquiry actually lives.

The key observation

Every BOM sheet contains its own enquiry. Before doing any engineering, the engineer transcribes what the customer asked into the header block — customer, SWL, SF, quantity, bag type, colour, required weight, and on the right-hand side the external and internal sizes, loop spec, top spec, bottom spec, doc pocket, liner, printing and packing.

That header is the customer's request, already normalised by a human. All 395 sheets have one. The raw WhatsApp message is missing; the content of it is not.

Step 1 — build the three gold layers from every sheet

One extraction pass over all 395 sheets produces three aligned records per sheet. This is mechanical, already prototyped, and needs no labelling.

tools/build_gold.py

for each BOM sheet:

  OrderSpec_gold    ← header block only
                       A3:C11  customer · order · ref · SWL · SF · qty · bag type · colour · BAG WT.
                       G3:L11  printing · ext size · int size · loop · top · bottom · doc · liner · packing
                     // NOTHING from the component table. This is the firewall.

  BuildSpec_gold    ← header + component table + INSTRACTION + tape/loom/needle blocks
                       family      ← taxonomy(bag_type_raw)
                       body_gsm    ← component "BODY PANEL".gsm
                       belt_gpm    ← component "LOOP".gsm
                       loop_*      ← parse header "4X25 · LL 60 CM · SL 40 CM"
                       has_*       ← component row present AND weight > 0
                       yarn_*      ← component "STITCHING"
                       stitch/fold ← INSTRACTION lines
                       rm_blend    ← MIXING line

  PhysicalBOM_gold  ← component table verbatim, with qty recovered by
                       reconciling recorded weight against unit weight
                     // used ONLY to test the renderer. Never a training target.
The firewall, and why it is non-negotiable

OrderSpec_gold must be built from the header block alone. If a single field leaks in from the component table, adapter A learns to predict engineering decisions from the enquiry, adapter B is trained on an input that will not exist at run time, and the whole evaluation becomes meaningless while looking excellent.

Enforce it in code, not by discipline: build_order_spec() receives only the header cell range and physically cannot see the rest of the sheet.

Step 2 — adapter B's training data is now finished

It is a direct join over the gold layers. No synthesis, no labelling, no judgement calls.

            "spec_fingerprint":"a3f9...", "integrity_flags":[]}

395 rows, immediately. Roughly 690 if the extractor is also run over BOM/BOM/Data, which holds workbooks not present in All_Things.

Two extra row types worth generating

  • Solver-feedback rows. For each sheet, take the true BuildSpec, perturb the body GSM by ±1 or ±2 catalogue steps, run the renderer to get the resulting weight gap, and train the model to move it back. Input carries a WeightFeedback block; output is the true BuildSpec. This is what teaches the model to close the loop rather than guess once. About 1,200 rows from 395 sheets, all machine-generated and exactly correct.
  • Infeasibility rows. Take real sheets and set a target weight below the lightest legal build. Correct output is a refusal object naming the blocking constraint. Around 200 rows. Without these the model will silently break the GSM floor to satisfy a bad target, which is the most dangerous failure this system has.

Step 3 — adapter A's data has to be built, and honestly labelled as synthetic

This is the part with real work in it. We have 395 gold OrderSpecs and almost no real enquiry text. So the enquiry text is generated from the OrderSpec — and every generated row is machine-verified before it is allowed into training.

tools/build_extractor_set.py — the round-trip guarantee

for each OrderSpec_gold:
    for style in [whatsapp_en, whatsapp_hinglish, email_formal, tds_table, phone_notes]:

        1. DROP fields at the rates measured in the corpus, so the synthetic
           enquiry is as incomplete as a real one:
                 liner absent 78.5% · doc pocket 44.6% · printing 43.0%
                 packing 17.0%      · target weight 9.1% · SWL 0.8%

        2. RENDER the surviving fields into prose with a large model.
           Inject the real noise found in the corpus:
                 PANNEL · CLOUSER · CIRCULAR NAG · LOOPBAFFLE (no space)
                 gr/m2 · 3" · 5:01 · 6:01/UN · 2.5kg / 2500 gm / 2.5 KGS

        3. VERIFY — re-extract from the generated text and compare
           field by field against the OrderSpec it came from.
                 identical      → KEEP
                 any mismatch   → DISCARD, do not repair, do not ship

        4. NEVER render from BuildSpec_gold. The customer did not
           choose the belt gramage; putting it in the enquiry teaches the
           extractor to read a field that will never be there.

Yield after verification is realistically 60–70%, giving roughly 1,200–1,400 verified synthetic rows from 395 sheets across five styles.

Then add the rows that cannot be synthesised, which are the ones that decide whether it works:

  • ~200 hard negatives, hand-constructed. Two size triples in one sentence where one is labelled internal. A spout size and a bag size in the same line. Negations — "without liner", "bina printing ke". A TDS-style Ø 40 CM × 50 CM HEIGHT. These are the exact cases a regex gets wrong, and the model must be shown them explicitly.
  • ~30 real documents, hand-labelled. The six SPEC JIEXP files, the seven COMPONENT sheets, the scans after OCR. These go entirely into evaluation and never into training. They are the only genuinely in-distribution samples we have, and spending them on training would leave nothing to measure against.
State this plainly to yourselves

Adapter B is trained on real engineering decisions. Adapter A is trained largely on reconstructed enquiries. The first is solid; the second is a scaffold that must be validated against the ~30 real documents and replaced with real enquiries as they accumulate. Do not let the two be described with the same confidence in any report.

Step 4 — deduplicate, then split by fingerprint

1. Deduplicate workbooks by content hash before anything else.
   DOMESTIC ENQUIRY.xlsx  appears in 2 locations (81 + 59 sheets)
   EXPORT ENQUIRY.xlsx    appears in 3 locations (33 + 44 + 31 sheets)
   Training on these teaches the model those bags are 3× more likely than they are.

2. Collapse exact duplicate (input, output) pairs to a single row.

3. Fingerprint each record:
   sha1(family | ext_L | ext_W | ext_H | swl | sf | top_type | bottom_type | target_weight)

4. Split the FINGERPRINT GROUPS, never the rows — stratified by family.
   train 70% · val 15% · test 15%
   A sheet and its near-twin from another workbook land on the same side of the split.

5. Assert zero leakage as a unit test that fails the build:
   assert not (set(train.fingerprints) & set(val.fingerprints))
   assert not (set(train.instructions) & set(val.instructions))

The previous dataset split by workbook, which was the right instinct, and still leaked 49% of validation rows verbatim into training — because the same enquiry file exists three times under different paths. Fingerprint grouping is what actually fixes it.

Serving — the grammar matters more than the fine-tune

Serve under vLLM with XGrammar, passing the JSON Schema generated from the Pydantic model as guided_json. Sub-40 µs per token, effectively free.

This makes a structurally invalid or out-of-enum answer impossible to emit, not merely unlikely. The two failure modes are then attacked separately: the grammar handles form, the weights handle content. Combined with the validation gate, an illegal BuildSpec cannot reach the renderer by any path.

Encode as much as possible into the schema itself rather than checking it afterwards:

body_gsm       : enum[65,70,75,80,85,90,95,100,103,105,110,120,122,125,130,135,140,
                      143,145,150,155,160,165,170,175,180,185,190,195,200,205,210,
                      215,220,225,230,240,245,260,370]     // the 40 values actually woven
belt_width_mm  : enum[40,45,50,60,70,72,75,80,100]
belt_gpm       : enum[22,25,28,30,32,35,40,42,45,50,55,60,65]
family         : enum[u_panel, circular, four_panel, four_panel_baffle, two_panel,
                      single_loop, two_loop, omega, tunnel, tubular, box, container]
loop_qty       : enum[1,2,4]
static_type    : enum[A,B,C,D]
dust_proof     : enum[NONE, SINGLE, DOUBLE, TRIPLE]

// What the grammar CANNOT express, so the gate must still check it:
//   body_gsm >= envelope.gsm_min          (depends on this order's SWL/SF)
//   belt_gpm inside envelope.belt_band    (depends on this order's load)
//   mandatory component set for family    (depends on the chosen family)
// Defence in depth: grammar for form, gate for consistency.

07 Evaluation and ship gates

Build the eval before the training script. If you change the retrieval-GSM rule and the fine-tune at the same time, you will never know which one worked.

Three held-out sets, split by fingerprint, stratified by family

  • eval_clean — ~60 sheets with no integrity flags, in-distribution
  • eval_hard — ~30 sheets: missing SWL or target weight, unusual families, accessory-heavy bags
  • eval_real — the ~30 hand-labelled real PDFs and scans. Never trained on. This is the only set that measures the actual job.
  • eval_regression — 20 fixed U-panel cases, including the 85×85×85 reference. These must never regress.

Four baselines that must be beaten, or nothing ships

  1. The deterministic rule engine alone, no model anywhere.
  2. Rule engine + retrieval fusion, still no model.
  3. Base Qwen3-4B, no fine-tuning, few-shot with 8 examples, under the same grammar. This is the one people skip and then regret. It costs nothing and is often startlingly close. Run it in week one.
  4. The current regex normaliser, for adapter A specifically.

Measure the target-weight inversion change on its own, first. It is worth more than either adapter and needs no GPU. If you change it at the same time as the fine-tune, the attribution is lost permanently.

08 Build order

Steps 1–5 need no GPU and deliver most of the measurable gain.

  1. Lock the answers into rule tables2 days · no GPU

    Every confirmed rule from the questionnaire becomes a row in rules/registry/*.json with its source and rule level attached. Geometry, GSM charts, belt bands, mandatory components, loom widths, canonical component names.

  2. Build the renderer and test it against all 395 sheets4 days · no GPU

    Feed each sheet's own BuildSpec_gold in and check the output equals PhysicalBOM_gold. This isolates the arithmetic completely: if it does not reproduce history exactly, no model can save it.

  3. Add the solver loop and the feasibility gate3 days · no GPU

    With a deterministic GSM chooser first — invert the weight formula, snap to catalogue, floor at the chart. Measure. This alone should take median weight error under 3%.

  4. Build the gold layers and enforce the firewall3 days

    build_gold.py producing OrderSpec / BuildSpec / PhysicalBOM per sheet, with the header-only constraint enforced structurally and a test that fails if it is violated.

  5. Deduplicate, fingerprint, split1 day

    Content-hash the workbooks, collapse duplicate pairs, fingerprint-group, stratify, and add the zero-leakage assertion to the test suite.

  6. Adapter B — the valuable one3 days

    Its data already exists. Generate the solver-feedback and infeasibility rows, train, serve under grammar, measure against baselines 1 and 2.

  7. Build the extractor set and hand-label the real documents4 days

    Round-trip-verified synthesis, 200 hard negatives, then the ~30 real PDFs and scans labelled by hand into eval_real.

  8. Adapter A2 days

    Train, serve under grammar, and gate hard on hallucination rate against eval_real. Keep the regex normaliser wired as an automatic fallback.

  9. Output writer and audit trail3 days

    The four-block sheet in the plant's own layout, plus per-field provenance. Show the engineer which lines differ from the nearest historical bag — that is what earns the first five minutes of trust.

  10. Shadow runongoing

    Generate a BOM for every real enquiry alongside the engineer for a month. Do not show him the output until he has finished his own. Every disagreement is either a bug or a rule nobody has written down yet, and both are worth more than any further training.

09 What would break this

Known risks, in order of how much damage they do
RiskMitigation
The firewall leaks — a BuildSpec field reaches OrderSpec_goldStructural, not procedural: the builder function cannot see the component table. Plus a test asserting OrderSpec fields are a strict subset of header cells.
Synthetic enquiries teach paraphrase artefacts rather than FIBC vocabularyMachine-verify every row by round-trip. Cap synthetic at ~70% of the set. Keep eval_real 100% real and gate on it, not on eval_clean.
Model breaks the GSM floor to satisfy an impossible targetThe 200 infeasibility training rows, the envelope in the prompt, the gate check, and a hard assertion in the writer. Four independent layers.
Solver oscillates — overshoots, corrects, overshootsPass history in WeightFeedback so the model sees what it already tried; cap at 3; fall back to the deterministic inversion result if it has not converged.
The engineer's answers contradict the data — e.g. he says +10 always, but 12 sheets say +8His answer wins as the rule; the 12 sheets get an integrity flag and are excluded from geometry training. Record the disagreement rather than silently picking one.
Nobody retrains itBoth adapters retrain in under an hour. Script them as tools/train_a.py and tools/train_b.py next to build_gold.py, and run them whenever the corpus grows.
Rule tables drift from the modelAdapter A extracts customer intent only and encodes no rule tables, so registry edits never invalidate it. Adapter B sees the envelope as input rather than memorising it, so a chart change is a data change, not a retrain.

The one thing to start today

Keep the raw enquiries. Every WhatsApp message, forwarded email, photographed spec sheet and scanned TDS, exactly as received, filed against the order number it became. Twenty real ones are worth more than a thousand synthesised, and every week that passes without collecting them is a week of the only training data that cannot be reconstructed.
