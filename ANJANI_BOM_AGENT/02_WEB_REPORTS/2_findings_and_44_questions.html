Anjani Interweave Pvt Ltd FIBC BOM Generation System Findings + questions for the engineer 10 Aug 2026

What the sheets already tell us —
and what only the engineer can

I parsed 395 real BOM sheets from All_Things and tested every candidate rule against them. Most of the geometry is now settled to 90%+ certainty. What is left is not arithmetic — it is judgement, and it is concentrated in about forty questions. Answer these and the model has something worth learning.

01 What I actually read

107 workbooks, 681 sheets, of which 395 carry a real component table. One workbook (FIBC PRODUCTION DEC-2025.xlsx) is a renamed .xls and cannot be opened; nine other files are .doc/.pdf/images.

The format is remarkably consistent. Every sheet is the same document:

  • Header — customer, order, ref, SWL, SF, qty, bag type, colour, bag weight; and on the right, printing, external size, internal size, loop, top, bottom, doc pocket, liner, packing.
  • Component table — a fixed 15-row template: SR NO. · COMPONENT · GSM · LAMI/UL · FABRIC COLOR · FABRIC WIDTH · CUT LENGTH · CUT PIECE WEIGHT · REMARK, closed by GRAND TOTAL.
  • INSTRACTION block — 3–6 free-text production lines. Fold type, stitch type, yarn spec, packing, tolerance.
  • TAPE PLANT / LOOM DEPARTMENT / NIDDLE LOOM — denier, tenacity, GPD, elongation, tape width; fabric GSM, warp/weft strength, mesh; belt strength in KGF, GPM, yarn denier.
  • MIXING — PP 80% · CC 8% · UV 1.5% · RP 12.5%.
This changes the scope in a good way

The BOM is not a materials list. It is a complete manufacturing instruction that runs tape plant → loom → needle loom → cutting → stitching → finishing → packing. The component table is the middle third of it.

Per your instruction I have dropped the costing block entirely. The tape/loom/needle-loom and INSTRACTION blocks I have kept, because they are core BOM, not price.

The loop formula is solved

The header writes the loop as 4X25 · LL 60 CM · SL 40 CM — that is quantity × free height, then the two attachment lengths. Cut length is 2×25 + 60 + 40 = 150 cm, and the weight is GPM × cut/100 × 4. It reproduces exactly on 197 of 226 testable sheets.

This matters because the earlier system used LL + SL + 50 and scored 31% — it was simply the wrong formula. Corner-loop geometry was never the hard problem it appeared to be.

Quantity is not written down anywhere

There is no QTY column. It is recoverable only by dividing the recorded weight by the unit weight.

Body panel resolves to qty 1 on 43% of sheets, qty 2 on 39%, qty 4 on 18% — that is U-panel, circular (two tube walls) and 4-panel respectively. Side panel is always 2, loop always 4, baffle always 4. This is reliable, but it is inference, and it is the single most fragile step in reading these sheets.

Across every sheet that states both, the component total lands within 2% of the stated bag weight on 78% of orders and within 1% on 61%. One instruction line, appearing verbatim on seven sheets, states the tolerance outright: BAG WT +/-2 %. The engineer is solving for a number, not reporting one.

The vocabulary is enormous

U PANNEL, U-PANNEL, U+2, U+2 PANEL, U PANEL BAG — five spellings of one construction. D/S, D SPOUT, DS SPOUT, DISCHARGE SPOUT, BOTTOM SPOUT, D/ SPOUT — six labels for one component. And genuine typos that carry meaning: CIRCULAR NAG, 4-CORNER LOOPBAFFLE BAG, IRISH CLOUSER.

This is exactly what a fine-tuned model handles well and a regex never will — but somebody has to confirm the canonical list once.

04 Domain notes from outside the factory

Checked against published FIBC practice so the questions below are asked in the industry's own terms, not invented ones.

  • Construction families are standard industry terms: U-panel (one U-piece forms bottom and two sides, plus two panels), 4-panel (four pieces sewn to a square), circular/tubular (woven as a tube, seams only at top and bottom). Shape retention runs 4-panel > U-panel > circular; cost runs the other way. Baffle / Q-bag / formstable all name the same thing — internal corner panels that stop the bag bulging.
  • Safety factor is not a free choice. 5:1 means single-trip; 6:1 is required for multi-trip and for UN-certified bags. ISO 21898 is the governing standard for FIBC construction and testing. This means SF is dictated by the customer's use case, which explains why it is almost never missing from your sheets (absent on only 3 of 395).
  • Type A/B/C/D is a static-protection classification, not a construction one. Type A = no protection; Type B = low breakdown voltage, dry flammable powders only; Type C = conductive threads, must be grounded; Type D = static-dissipative, no grounding needed. Your sheets record this as "Static Protection Type" and as bag types like type - C bag and U+2 CORNER LOOP CONDUCTIV.
  • Manufacturing sequence — tape extrusion (PP + CaCO₃ + UV masterbatch, drawn to denier and tenacity), weaving on flat or circular looms, separate needle-loom production of lifting webbing, then cutting, printing, stitching and finishing. Your TAPE PLANT / LOOM / NIDDLE LOOM blocks map onto exactly these stages, which is why they belong in the BOM.
  • Denier is grams per 9,000 m of yarn — so the 3200 DNR / 3600 DNR figures in the stitching row are a yarn thickness spec, not a weight.

None of this contradicts your sheets. It does mean the system can safely use standard terminology in its output, which will matter when a European customer reads the spec.

05 Questions for the engineer

Forty-four questions. Twelve are marked blocking — the system cannot be built correctly without them. The rest improve accuracy. Each carries the evidence that prompted it, so the engineer can see exactly which sheets are being asked about.

A · Classification and defaults

Because misclassifying the construction poisons every number downstream

A1

When the customer says only "open top, flat bottom" and gives no construction — you default to U+2. Is that always the default, or does it change with bag size, SWL, product density, or whether it is an export order?blocking

Our example query has no construction stated and was built as U-Panel corner loop. Across the corpus the family split is u_panel 174 · circular 113 · baffle 70 · 4-panel 11 — so circular is common enough that "always U+2" needs confirming.
A2

What makes you choose corner loop versus cross-corner loop? Is it the construction, the SWL, the customer, or something about how they lift?blocking

Circular bags are cross-corner on 71 of 113 sheets; U-panel is corner loop on essentially all. But 4 PANNEL BAFFLE appears with both. The choice drives a completely different GSM table and belt table.
A3

Are these all the same construction, or do any differ? U PANNEL · U-PANNEL · U PANEL BAG · U+2 · U+2 PANEL · U+2 PANNEL

Five spellings, 174 sheets. Assuming they are one family; if U+2 ever means something different from U PANNEL, the whole u_panel group is contaminated.
A4

Is DOUBLE LAYER U-PANNEL BAG a separate construction, or U-panel with a modifier? Same question for U+2 WITH DOUBLE LAYER (STEVE' DOOR).

4 sheets each. Body-panel implied quantity is 2 on some U-panel sheets, which is what a double layer would look like — but I cannot tell it apart from an extraction error.
A5

Which of these do we need to support, and which are one-offs we can leave out of v1? H BOTTOM BAG · OMEGA · TUNNEL · TUBELAR WITH TOP HEMMING · CONTAINER LINER BAG · 2 LOOP · SINGLE LOOP · CIRCULAR ROUND WITH FULL RUNNING LOOP

Between 2 and 14 sheets each — enough to name, not enough to learn a rule from.
A6

Does Q-bag always mean 4-panel baffle, or can a Q-bag be built on a circular body?

4 PANNEL BAFFLE BAG ( Q BAG ) appears 7 times, 4 PANNEL WITH BAFFLE BAG ( Q BAG ) 6 times. Industry usage treats Q-bag as any formstable baffled FIBC, which is broader.

B · Cut sizes and seam allowances

Because one sheet disagrees with the other 138 and it happens to be the example we are building against

B1

U-panel body cut length: is the standard 2H + L + 10 or 2H + L + 8? What makes it change?blocking

+10 on 138 of 174 sheets (79%), +8 on 12 (7%). But the 85×85×85 sheet you gave me as the reference uses +8 (cut 263 = 170 + 85 + 8). By customer: EXPORT ENQUIRY 29/31 use +10; VIJAYNAGAR 3/4 use +8; DOMESTIC uses +3, +6 and +8.
B2

Are all cut sizes computed from the external dimensions, or does any component work off internal?blocking

Every rule I fitted works off external and lands on a clean +8/+10. Your sheets state both sizes (external usually internal + 4 to 5 cm). If any component is actually cut off internal, that rule is wrong by 4–5 cm and I would not detect it.
B3

Confirm the fixed U-panel set: body width = W + 8, side panel = (L + 8) × (H + 8), quantity 2. Is the +8 four centimetres of seam on each edge?

94%, 94% and 98% agreement respectively. The 3_Corrected_UPanel sheet in your variance analysis says "4 cm seam each long edge", which fits.
B4

Circular body: is the flat woven width exactly W + L with no seam allowance because it comes off the loom as a tube? And is the body always counted as 2 walls?

Mode of (width − W) is 90 on square 90×90 bags, i.e. width = W + L. Body implied quantity is 2 on 39% of all sheets, which is close to the circular share (113/395).
B5

Why do circular top/bottom panels use +10 while baffle top/bottom use +8?

Circular top/bottom: +10 on 86% of 94. Baffle top/bottom: +8 on 61% of 70. Consistent enough to be deliberate, but I do not know the reason and cannot generalise it to a new family without one.
B6

Do you round the computed fabric width up to an available loom width, or does the loom get set to whatever the calculation says?blocking

On the 85×85×85 sheet, body and side are both 93 cm — one loom setting serves both. That looks deliberate. But if loom width is a hard constraint, the whole geometry needs a snap-to-available-width step; if not, it must not have one. What are the actual available widths?
B7

Filling and discharge spouts — what are the width and cut formulas? Is the stated size (e.g. 40×50 CM) a diameter or a flat width, and which of the two numbers is the height?

Observed: FS 40×50 → 55 × 137.6 cut; DS 35×50 with iris → 85 × 122; DS 40×50 iris → 85 × 137.6. I can see the pattern but cannot pin the formula, and the same stated size gives different widths on different sheets.
B8

How much extra fabric and tie length does each closure add — open/tied, iris, petal, pyjama?

Iris rope cut lengths observed: 165, 195, 175 cm on otherwise similar bags. Tie rows run 125–195 cm at 6–7 g/m.

C · GSM selection — the biggest gap

Because the same bag was built at 125 and at 210

C1

Two orders, same construction, same 90×90×110 cm, same SWL 1000 kg, same 5:1 — one built at 125 GSM, one at 210. What is the difference between those two orders?blocking

This is the question the whole project turns on. 24 of 108 identical-spec groups disagree on body GSM. If the answer is "the customer's product" or "export versus domestic" or "how many trips they reuse it", that field has to be captured in the enquiry and fed to the model.
C2

Do you work forward from SWL and safety factor to a GSM, or backward from the customer's required bag weight to whatever GSM lands on it?blocking

The data says backward: the component total is within 2% of the stated bag weight on 78% of 350 sheets. Inverting the weight formula predicts the chosen GSM with a median error of 0.00%, against 3.33% for the load-chart cell. If that is really how you work, the software should work that way too.
C3

Your GSM- GPM VS SWL-SF.xlsx has tables for two-panel, omega, 4-panel baffle (both loop types) and tunnel — but no U-panel table. Which table do you read for a U+2 bag?blocking

U-panel is 44% of the corpus and has no chart. An earlier analysis assumed the 4-panel corner-loop column (140 GSM at 751–1000 kg, 5:1), which would make the 85×85×85 bag's 150 GSM a +7% margin. Please confirm or correct.
C4

SWL of exactly 1000 kg falls in both the 751–1000 and 1000–1250 rows. Which one wins?

1000 kg is the single most common SWL in the corpus, so this boundary is hit constantly. The two rows differ by 15–20 GSM.
C5

Where the chart gives a range — 145–150 GSM, 155–160, 230–240 — what makes you take the top rather than the bottom?

On the 85×85×85 bag the belt chart said 30–35 GPM and you took 35, the top of the band. Is that habitual, or was there a reason on that order?
C6

If the customer states a GSM and it is below the chart minimum for their SWL and safety factor — what do you do?blocking

This decides whether the software may ever go below the chart. It is the safety question in the whole system.
C7

The remark column writes lamination as 70+20 LAMINATED — base fabric plus coating. Is the GSM column always the total (90), never the base?

70+20 LAMINATED appears on 184 rows, and the GSM column reads 90 alongside it. Confirming, because if it is ever the base figure the weight is out by 20–30%.
C8

What does 175+30 UNLAMINATED mean? And is 15+90+15 both-side lamination?

12 rows say a lamination figure and then "UNLAMINATED". 9 rows use the three-part form. Both look deliberate rather than typed wrong.
C9

Which components take a different GSM from the body as a matter of course, and what governs each — top panel, spouts, baffle, bottom?

Observed: top panel 80–120 laminated, spouts almost always 90 (70+20), baffle 130 both-side laminated, circular bottom heavier than body per the chart. Looks like a fixed policy rather than a per-order choice.

F · Stitching, yarn and production instructions

Because these lines are part of the BOM and the model will have to write them

F1

Is the sewing-yarn weight a formula, or do you pick a round number from experience?

Yarn weight divided by bag height has a median of 0.467 g/cm — close to the 0.45 rule of thumb — but the tenth-to-ninetieth percentile spans 0.33 to 0.72. The values used are round: 55 g on 45 sheets, 60 g on 62, 65 g on 35. That looks chosen, not calculated.
F2

What decides the stitch specification — HR+DR, overlock + chain, double chain, single fold, butterfly?

All appear as instruction lines, up to 16 times each. Is it construction, customer, or product?
F3

Yarn denier appears as 2200, 3000, 3200 and 3600, sometimes two in one bag (SAFTY IN WHITE 2200 DNR AND OVER LOCK WITH 3600 DNR BLUE). What selects them, and what does the colour convention mean?

6 sheets use that exact two-yarn line. Blue and white recur throughout — I assume white is safety stitch and blue overlock, but that is a guess.
F4

Fold type — J-fold, out-fold, box J-fold, top hemming — is this determined by construction, or is it a customer preference?

U+2 PANEL BAG WITH J FOLD BOX STITCHING AND TOP OUT FOLD on 12 sheets; CIRCULAR BAG WITH TOP AND BOTTOM J-FOLD on 6. It looks construction-linked but not exclusively.
F5

Should the generated BOM produce the INSTRACTION lines, the TAPE PLANT / LOOM / NIDDLE LOOM specs and the MIXING recipe — or does the plant fill those in later?

They are present on most sheets but not all, and the mixing recipe is legible on only a handful. If they are part of the deliverable, we need the rules; if they are added downstream, we can leave them out and the scope shrinks usefully.
F6

What drives the raw-material blend? Observed PP 88.5 / CC 10 / UV 1.5 and PP 80 / CC 8 / UV 1.5 / RP 12.5.

Is RP (recycled) allowed only for domestic orders? Does UV always sit at 1.5%? Does CaCO₃ change with GSM?

Is ±2% the standard tolerance on bag weight? Does it change by customer or by whether the order is export?blocking

BAG WT +/-2 % appears verbatim on 7 sheets, and 78% of all 350 measurable sheets land inside 2%. If ±2% is the rule, it becomes the solver's stopping condition.

06 How to use this

The twelve blocking questions — A1, A2, B1, B2, B6, C1, C2, C3, C6, D1, E1, E3, G1, G2, G3 — are worth a single sitting with the engineer, in that order. They are not independent: B1 and B2 together fix the geometry, C1 through C6 fix material selection, and G1 through G3 define the solver.

The rest can be answered on paper at his own pace. Where he says "it depends", that is the most valuable answer in the document — it names a field we are not currently capturing, and every such field is one the model will otherwise have to guess.

Two things worth asking him to do while he answers:

  • Walk one enquiry out loud. Take a recent order, and have him narrate every decision from reading the message to signing the sheet — including the ones he makes without noticing. That transcript is worth more than any single answer here.
  • Keep the next twenty raw enquiries. Screenshots, forwarded emails, WhatsApp text, scans — untouched. We have 395 finished BOMs and almost no examples of what the model will actually be given as input.

When the answers come back, the immediate effect is concrete: the geometry rules become hard-coded tables, the GSM decision becomes a solver against the target weight with the chart as a floor, and the model's job narrows to the specific judgements the engineer has just described. That is a much smaller and much more learnable target than "generate a BOM".
