The most important single answer

C1. The 68% GSM spread on identical specifications is explained by the product the bag will carry, and the customer states it. That was the one thing nothing in the sheets could explain. 02 Four rules that fail against the data

I tested every stated rule on all 395 sheets. These four do not reproduce. His answer is the rule going forward — but the disagreement is large enough that writing code on it without one more sentence would be reckless.

① B1 — the +8 / +10 body cut

He says: "2H+L+8 is the standard, but we use 2H+L+10 when the body fabric GSM is less than 120 and the bag height is above 100 cm."

< 120≤ 100 cm0——+8 — no data

The conditional cell contains a single sheet. In the two cells where the rule predicts +8, the plant used +10 on 72% and 93% of bags. Even the 85×85×85 reference sheet he supplied — GSM 150, height 85, so squarely in the "+8" cell — is one of only four sheets in 58 that actually uses +8.

Needs one sentence

Ask him directly: "Your 395 historical U-panel sheets use +10 on 79% of bags including almost every one at GSM ≥ 120. Is +8 a new standard you have moved to, or should the system use +10 and treat +8 as the exception?" Every U-panel bag's body weight depends on the answer — roughly 3 g at 150 GSM, which is inside the ±2% tolerance but shifts every bag the same way.

② F1 — yarn weight per family

He gives four constants. Two hold, two do not.

③ D5 — belt strength, 1 gram carries 35 kg

Applied as gpm × 35 × 4 loops ≥ SWL × SF, this is satisfied on only 39.3% of 321 testable sheets. Shortfalls include ordinary production bags — SWL 1000 at 5:1 with a 30 g/m belt gives 4,200 kg against 5,000 needed.

The likely resolution: each corner loop has two load-bearing legs, so the capacity is gpm × 35 × 4 × 2. That makes the same bag 8,400 kg against 5,000 — and it matches the published guidance that all four loops carry in tandem. One sentence confirms it, and then this becomes an excellent hard validator.

④ B8 — iris tie length

D × 3.14 + 38 is exact on the cases I can verify by hand — a 40 cm spout gives 165 cm, a 50 cm spout gives 195 cm, both matching production sheets — and the 38 cm decomposes exactly as he describes (two ties, 10 cm outside each side, two knots of 9 cm). But swept across the corpus it only lands within 2 cm on 33% of 116 sheets.

The most likely cause is my own parsing: the diameter I read from the header is not always the spout the iris tie belongs to, and petal closures are mixed in. This one is probably a measurement artefact rather than a wrong rule — but it should be re-tested after the extractor is rebuilt, before the formula is trusted.

04 The architecture, final

Two stages removed, one added, and the solver reversed. Everything the model does is still a choice from a finite set; everything numeric is still code.

The decision algorithm, exactly

STEP 1 — establish the load
  if SWL stated                        → use it
  elif product_contents AND volume     → SWL = volume_m³ × bulk_density(product)   // G6
                                          bulk density from the National Bulk Bag table
  else                                 → CLARIFY: "SWL or product + fill volume required"
  if SF absent                         → CLARIFY: "single-trip (5:1) or multi-trip (6:1)?"

STEP 2 — classify   family, loop type, top, bottom          // deterministic, §3

STEP 3 — choose the mode                                     // C2
  target_weight stated  →  BACKWARD   solve for a build that lands within ±2%
  target_weight absent  →  FORWARD    GSM from chart, belt from chart, report the weight

STEP 4 — BACKWARD mode, knobs in the engineer's order        // G3
  pass 1   adjust loop GPM       within the belt band, honouring capacity >= SWL×SF
  pass 2   adjust loop cut       via free height / LL / SL, within observed ranges
  pass 3   adjust body GSM       only within market-standard values for the load
  still outside ±2%  →  do NOT force it. Emit both options and CLARIFY.  // G2

STEP 5 — the customer-override rule                          // C6
  customer stated a GSM below the chart minimum
      → build at the stated GSM
      → emit a WARNING, not a rejection
      → add to the clarification list: "GSM {x} is below our {y} standard for
         SWL {swl} at {sf}. Confirm you want us to proceed."

STEP 6 — the conflict case (the 85×85×85 enquiry)            // G2
  target weight and stated GSM cannot both hold
      → compute BOTH builds
      → present both, with the weight and the GSM each implies
      → CLARIFY: "proceed against bag weight, or against the specified GSM?"
What this means for the 720 g example

My earlier report called that enquiry "infeasible". With C6 and G2 answered, that was too strong. The correct behaviour is: build both — 878 g at the customer's 150 GSM, and ~112 GSM if 720 g is the binding constraint — state that 112 GSM is below the 140 GSM standard for SWL 1000 at 5:1, and ask which one they want. The system does not refuse and does not silently pick. It presents the trade and requests a decision.

05 Data and fine-tuning — what changed

The data plan from the previous report stands. Three amendments follow from the answers.

1 · OrderSpec gains the field that explains everything

product_contents — free text, plus a derived bulk_density_kg_m3 when the product matches the National Bulk Bag table. This is the answer to C1, and without it the model cannot learn why one 90×90×110 bag was 125 GSM and another 210.

It also feeds retrieval: two bags for the same product should rank closer than two bags of the same size for different products.

2 · Deduplicate by content, per H1

He was explicit: "Do not learn from duplicates. If the inquiry is the same and the content is the same, the agent should proceed accordingly." Content-hash the workbooks first — DOMESTIC ENQUIRY.xlsx appears in two locations, EXPORT ENQUIRY.xlsx in three — then collapse identical (input, output) pairs, then fingerprint-group the split.

3 · A third training target: the clarification list

G5 asks for the agent to identify what is missing and raise it. That is a generated output, so it needs supervision. It is derivable for free: for every historical sheet, compute which OrderSpec fields were not_specified, and emit the questions the rule book says are needed for those gaps. ~395 rows, zero labelling, and it makes the "ask, don't guess" behaviour trainable rather than hoped-for.

From the engineer — five short questions

  1. The +8 / +10 conflict (§2①). His rule predicts +8 in cells where the corpus uses +10 on 72% and 93% of bags. Which governs going forward?
  2. Baffle and single-loop yarn (§2②). Stated 72 g and 35 g; corpus medians are 100 g and 25 g.
  3. Belt capacity (§2③). Does each corner loop count as two load-bearing legs? At ×4 the rule fails on 61% of bags; at ×8 it works.
  4. The U+2 GSM table (C3). He said a fresh one will be prepared. Until it exists, U-panel — 44% of all bags — has no chart, and the forward mode cannot run for it.
  5. The belt strength sheet (D5) and the liner cut-size formula (E7), both referred to as "already shared" but not present in this folder.

Two operational asks, unchanged and still the highest-value items

  • Keep the next twenty raw enquiries. He agreed to try. This is the only training data that cannot be reconstructed, and every week without it is a week lost.
  • Have him narrate one enquiry end to end. Including the decisions he makes without noticing. Several of the four conflicts above would likely dissolve in five minutes of listening.

07 Build order

Nothing before step 4 needs a GPU, and steps 1–4 alone should take median weight error under 3%.

  1. Rule registry from the answers2 days

    Every confirmed rule in §3 becomes JSON with its source, rule level and corpus agreement attached. The four disputed rules go in flagged needs_confirmation and fail loudly rather than silently picking a side.

  2. Renderer, tested against all 395 sheets4 days

    Feed each sheet's own BuildSpec in; the output must equal its component table. Spout formulas should reproduce at ~94% immediately. This isolates the arithmetic completely.

  3. Classifier, SWL derivation, envelope3 days

    Deterministic family/loop/top rules, the bulk-density lookup, and the GSM and belt bands. Digitise the National Bulk Bag density table — 28 pages, product name plus lbs/ft³ plus g/cm³.

  4. Solver in the engineer's knob order, plus the clarification engine4 days

    Loop GPM, then loop cut, then body GSM. Both modes. The conflict path that emits two builds and a question. Measure against the 350 sheets that state a target weight.

  5. Gold layers, firewall, dedup, fingerprint split4 days

    OrderSpec built from the header block alone, enforced structurally. Content-hash dedup per H1. Zero-leakage assertion in the test suite.

  6. Adapter B, then adapter A6 days

    B first — its data is already real and complete. A second, gated hard on hallucination rate against the ~30 real documents.

  7. Output writer and audit3 days

    Four-block sheet, construction type first, then weight and GSM, then the rest. Per-field provenance. The clarification list as a co-equal deliverable.

  8. Shadow runone month

    Generate a BOM for every real enquiry alongside the engineer, without showing him until he has finished his own. Every disagreement is either a bug or a rule nobody has written down — and both are worth more than further training.

— In one paragraph

The BOM agent reads an enquiry in any format and extracts only what the customer actually said. It derives the load from bulk density if the customer gave a product and a volume instead of an SWL. It classifies the construction by fixed rule — U+2 unless a belt of 70 mm or more says circular. A fine-tuned Qwen adapter then makes roughly seventy engineering decisions from a closed vocabulary, informed by the three nearest bags Anjani has actually built and the chart bands for this load. Deterministic code turns those decisions into every number on the sheet. If the customer named a target weight, a solver adjusts loop gramage, then loop cut length, then body GSM until the bag lands within ±2%. A gate recomputes every line and refuses anything structurally wrong. And where the enquiry contradicts itself, or the chart, or physics, the agent does not decide alone — it builds both options and hands the engineer the exact question to put to the customer. That last behaviour is the one the engineer asked for by name, and it is what separates a calculator from something worth trusting.
