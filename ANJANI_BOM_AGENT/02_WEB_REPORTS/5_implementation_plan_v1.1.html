Why the +8 / +10 reversal is safe

He ruled +8 against 79% of his own history. Two things make that fine: the cost is 2.79 g on an 880 g bag — 0.32%, well inside ±2%; and the model never emits a cut length. Cut length comes from a rule table in the renderer. Flipping +8 to +10 is a one-line JSON edit and a test re-run. No retraining, no data rebuild. That is the architecture paying for itself.

03 Architecture

Two stages deleted since the last draft — loom-width snapping, and the separate infeasibility path. Nothing is truly infeasible now, because the customer can always override.

The decision algorithm, exactly

STEP 1 — establish the load
  SWL stated                            -> use it
  product + fill volume given           -> SWL = volume_m3 x bulk_density(product)
  neither                               -> CLARIFY, never guess
  SF absent                             -> CLARIFY: single-trip (5:1) or multi-trip (6:1)?

STEP 2 — classify   family · loop type · top · bottom     deterministic

STEP 3 — choose the mode
  PO states a bag weight                -> BACKWARD  target = PO x 0.99, +/-2%
  PO states none                        -> FORWARD   chart GSM, chart belt, report weight

STEP 4 — BACKWARD, knobs in the engineer's order
  pass 1  adjust loop GPM   within the belt band, capacity >= SWL x SF
  pass 2  adjust loop cut   free height / LL / SL, inside observed ranges
  pass 3  adjust body GSM   only within market-standard values for the load
  still outside +/-2%  ->  do NOT force it. Emit both builds and CLARIFY.

STEP 5 — the customer-override rule
  customer stated a GSM below the chart minimum
      -> build at the stated GSM
      -> WARNING, not a rejection
      -> clarification: "GSM {x} is below our {y} standard for SWL {swl} at {sf}.
                         Confirm you want us to proceed."
Correction to my earlier report

I called the 720 g enquiry "infeasible". With C6 and G2 answered that was too strong. The correct behaviour: build both — 878 g at the customer's 150 GSM, and ~112 GSM if 720 g binds — note that 112 is below the 140 standard, and ask which one they want. Not refuse. Not silently pick. Present the trade and request a decision.

Two adapters because they are trained to do opposite things. A must emit not_specified for a missing field; B must fill it in. 06 The engineer's notebook

Sixty pages of his own training notes — written for himself, not in reply to a questionnaire, which makes it the most candid source in the project. It closes two open items, adds geometry for eight more families, and supplies the plant-floor formulas that were previously blank.

Two open items closed

O3 — belt gramage. Resolved.

required_GPM = (SWL × SF) / (loop_qty × 35) — no leg factor. A straight divide by the number of loops. His worked example: 1000 kg at 6:1 → 6000 kg → ÷4 loops = 1500 kg → ÷35 = 42.8 g → "approxly 40–45 gram loop."

Corpus check on 321 bags: median deviation 16%, and only 45% sit at or above the computed figure. So it is a design target he rounds to an available belt, not a limit. V09 therefore moves from reject to warn — as a hard gate it would have rejected more than half of Anjani's own production history.

O2 — liner cut size. Resolved.

width = 2W + 5 · length = H + skirt + W/2 + spout_height + 10 knotting + 5 hemming. His example: 95×95×125 with an 80 cm skirt and a 40×50 D/S → 195 × 317 cm. And the weight line micron × L × W × 2 × 0.92 / 10000 confirms W3 exactly.

The governing standard is also settled. His notes cite ISO 21898 directly, with ASTM 154-G for UV. The "need to check" mark on the rules PDF is superseded — the output may now carry the standard.

— In one paragraph

The BOM agent reads an enquiry in any format and extracts only what the customer actually said, marking everything else not_specified. It derives the load from bulk density when the customer gave a product and a volume instead of an SWL. It classifies the construction by fixed rule — U+2 unless a belt of 70 mm or more says circular. A fine-tuned Qwen adapter then makes roughly seventy engineering decisions from a closed vocabulary, informed by the three nearest bags Anjani has actually built and the chart bands for this load. Deterministic code turns those decisions into every number on the sheet: W + 8 and 2H + L + 8 for a U-panel body, D × 3.14 + 12 for a spout, 2 × FH + LL + SL for a loop, 0.45 × H for the yarn. If the customer named a bag weight, a solver aims at 99% of it and adjusts loop gramage, then loop cut length, then body GSM until the bag lands within ±2%. A gate of twenty-one checks recomputes every line and refuses anything structurally wrong. And where the enquiry contradicts itself, or the chart, or physics, the agent does not decide alone — it builds both options and hands the engineer the exact question to put to the customer.
